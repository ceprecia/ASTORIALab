## What the course teaches

By the end, a learner should be able to distinguish individuals from species, calculate relative abundance, combine count records without double-counting species, explain sampling effort, and separate observed sample differences from claims about whole habitats.

## Four-week outline

| Week | Dates | Topic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | February 3 to 7 | Species counts, relative abundance and sampling effort |
| 2 | February 10 to 14 | Repeated samples and fair comparisons |
| 3 | February 17 to 21 | Checking ecological claims against supplied observations |
| 4 | February 24 to 28 | Writing an evidence-bounded ecology commentary |

Only Week 1 lesson content is supplied in this tutorial. Later topics establish the fictional course schedule.

Each quiz permits one 15-minute attempt. The availability window and attempt duration are separate constraints. Submit within the window.

For the Week 1 exercise, answer practice questions 1, 3 and 5 from the [lesson and practice file](lesson_and_practice.md#practice-questions), showing the reasoning. The other questions support practice for the quiz. The final commentary would interpret a new count table and explain its limits in at most 500 words. That later table is not supplied here.

## Working policies

- Use the supplied definitions and counts when answering an assessment. State any extra assumption explicitly.
- Show how totals and percentages were calculated. Keep counted individuals distinct from the number of species.
- For this fictional course, an assistant may explain the lesson, ask practice questions and critique a draft. The learner checks the reasoning and writes the submitted explanation.
- Extension decisions belong to the fictional teaching team. This tutorial contains no individual exceptions.
- Supplied lesson content controls the Week 1 answer key. All counts are synthetic; unmeasured habitat properties and ecological causes remain unknown.
