# Optional worked examples

[Read the main tutorial online](https://github.com/ceprecia/ASTORIALab/blob/main/tutorial.md)

These eight fictional projects are optional learning materials. You can follow
the main tutorial and set up ASTORIA without them. Choose one example that
interests you, try a conversation, then compare your result with an illustrative
output. You do not need to work through every system.

Read these examples online or [download the optional examples for v1.3.1](https://github.com/ceprecia/ASTORIALab/releases/download/v1.3.1/ASTORIA-examples-v1.3.1.zip).
They are separate from the starter vault.

## Choose an example

| System | Select during setup | Try this example when you want to… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Research Assistant](#research) | Basics | Plan a study and understand invented observations |
| [Janitor](#janitor) | Basics | Organize a mixed folder while keeping its notes and links |
| [ASTORIA System Builder](#system-builder) | Basics | Change a briefing and make a reusable labeling skill |
| [Writing](#writing) | Help me choose: Writing | Turn supplied evidence into a scientific report |
| [Study and Productivity](#study-productivity) | Help me choose: Study and Productivity | Learn a course topic and plan practice time |
| [Teaching](#teaching) | Help me choose: Teaching | Prepare a class, activity and feedback |
| [Engineering](#engineering) | Help me choose: Engineering | Review a circuit before building it |
| [PhD and Career](#phd-career) | Help me choose: PhD and Career | Prepare a milestone plan and advisor conversation |

**Basics** includes Research Assistant, Janitor and ASTORIA System Builder,
with their required shared helpers. An included helper does not select its
whole optional system. For example, Research's writing helpers do not install
the full Writing System. Select the system needed for the example you choose;
the available catalog and your installed selection are different things.

The projects and data are fictional. The illustrative outputs show possible
results, not work that an assistant, class, experiment or physical build has
actually completed. Engineering component specifications have their own
manufacturer sources, retained with that example.

## Work through an example

You can browse the linked materials online. To try a case, use a separate copy
of the starter and open it in Obsidian and your assistant. Complete setup with
the system required by your chosen example. Then copy the chosen inputs into
that practice vault and give your assistant access to them. Keep nested files
together, review proposed file moves or new skills before applying them, and
compare your result with the optional illustrative outputs.

## Research Assistant: a proofreading pilot

Research Companion helps you move from a scientific question to a plan and
interpretation. This example uses **Basics**, with no additional research
software or method group needed.

Copy the [Research inputs](research/inputs) and open the brief, protocol and
invented results.
They explore whether a quiet break changes performance on a proofreading task.

> Can you help me plan this study?

Read the proposed plan and discuss an unresolved design choice. Then ask what
the invented results can tell you, and check the summary against the eight
paired records. Each passage contains 12 planted errors. Does the answer keep
the original question, explain its arithmetic and distinguish invented
observations from evidence about real people?

Compare your answer with the [illustrative plan](research/illustrative_outputs/research_plan.md)
and [summary](research/illustrative_outputs/synthetic_summary.md).
Neither establishes a population effect or a causal finding.

To continue, change one design choice in a practice copy and ask how it affects
the plan. For your own research, bring your actual question, protocol and
approved observations.

## Janitor: a mixed exhibit folder

The Janitor System is part of **Basics**. For a general cleanup, ask Janitor
Companion to “Clean up my vault.” It routes the checks and presents proposed
fixes for your approval. Folder Janitor organizes selected files and repairs
their links. Vault Janitor checks the wider structure, and Skills Janitor
maintains the skills and their discovery views. Agent Janitor investigates
behavior such as agents falling back to outdated instructions and tests proposed
fixes before you approve them.

Copy the entire [Janitor inputs folder](janitor/inputs), including
its nested folders. Read the cleanup request and exhibit map, then look
through the mixed notes, flyer, reusable reference and kiosk files.

> Can you help me organize this folder?

Review the proposed moves before accepting them. Some notes have unclear
ownership, so leaving them in place is useful. The kiosk files belong together
as one working package. The exhibit map's text and dated decision should survive.

When you are happy with the plan, ask:

> Apply the moves we agreed on and check the links afterward.

Compare the result with the [example move plan](janitor/illustrative_outputs/move_plan.md)
and [checks](janitor/illustrative_outputs/verification_checklist.md).
The mixed-owner note and unidentified table stay where they are.

Next, try **Keep my structure** on another practice copy, or ask about one
folder whose real purpose and owners you know.

## ASTORIA System Builder: a briefing and a custom skill

System Builder is included in **Basics**. Its skills help you adapt the vault
to your preferences and turn a repeated task into a reusable workflow.

Copy the [System Builder inputs](system_builder/inputs).
Start with the fictional briefing answers:

> Can we draft a briefing from these answers?

Read the draft alongside the answers. It should leave unspecified details
unknown and use the fictional preferences rather than changing your real
briefing. Compare it with the [example excerpt](system_builder/illustrative_outputs/sample_briefing.md).

Next, open the label-making request:

> Could we turn this task into a skill?

Discuss the proposed skill and review it before creation in the practice vault.
This adds one chosen skill, rather than selecting an entire optional system.
The assistant checks for overlap and includes the supporting instructions.
Then open the packet inventory:

> Can you make the labels?

Compare the labels with the [finished example](system_builder/illustrative_outputs/labels.md).
You can also inspect the [illustrative skill folder](system_builder/illustrative_outputs/format-seed-labels)
and the format guide inside it;
that example folder is not already installed.

Try another procedure you repeat. An existing skill may already cover it; if
you import someone else's skill, keep its supporting files and attribution.

## Writing: an evidence-bounded scientific report

Choose the **Writing System** through **Help me choose**. Writing Companion
helps develop and review documents; Research's shared writing helpers do not
select this whole system.

Copy the [Writing inputs](writing/inputs) and open the brief, plant data and
rough report.
The report deliberately makes claims stronger than the fictional record supports.

> Which claims need better evidence?

Read the answer against the brief and table. Ask about any claim whose source
you cannot find. When you understand what can be supported, continue:

> Could you help me revise this report?

The revision should retain the synthetic-data label, supplied values and
limitations. Compare it with the [evidence ledger](writing/illustrative_outputs/evidence_ledger.md)
and [revised report](writing/illustrative_outputs/revised_report.md).
Look at how the wording changes when a claim has less support.

For your own report, supply approved methods and results, then use the same
conversation to examine the claims before polishing the prose.

## Study and Productivity: an ecology lesson and study week

Choose **Study and Productivity** through **Help me choose**. Study Companion
helps explain a topic and practice it; planning support helps you make time for
the work.

Copy the [Study inputs](study_productivity/inputs). This fictional foundational
ecology course uses invented species counts. Read the course note and syllabus,
then open the lesson and practice questions.

> Can you explain this lesson?

Try a practice question before asking to see the answer. Check that the
explanation distinguishes individuals, observed species richness and relative
abundance. Compare it with the [illustrative explanation and answers](study_productivity/illustrative_outputs/study_explanation_and_answers.md).
The counts support an arithmetic exercise, not findings about real habitats.

For the productivity example, share the weekly planning brief and syllabus:

> Can you help me plan my study week?

Check that the plan fits the supplied task estimates, deadlines and available
sessions. Compare it with the [example week](study_productivity/illustrative_outputs/weekly_plan.md).
This is a proposed plan; no study progress, calendar event or reminder has been
created.

Next, bring your own syllabus and current lesson. Share the actual course
sources when you want an explanation grounded in that course.

## Teaching: an instructor's week and feedback

Choose the **Teaching System** through **Help me choose**. Teaching Companion
helps prepare classes and feedback using an instructor's approved course sources.

Copy the [Teaching inputs](teaching/inputs). This time you
are the instructor for a different fictional course about clear procedures.
Read the course note, instructor syllabus and second week's sources:

> Can you help me plan this class?

Check that the plan addresses the learning objective and leaves students time
to attempt the activity. Compare it with the [example lesson plan and activity](teaching/illustrative_outputs/lesson_plan_and_activity.md).

Now open the activity rubric and fictional student work:

> How could I give useful feedback on this work?

Check the comments and proposed score against the rubric, then compare with
the [illustrative feedback](teaching/illustrative_outputs/feedback.md).
These are drafts for instructor review, not actual grades or course policy.

Keep the practice work in chat. To adapt it, bring your own approved course
materials, objectives and rubric, and review the proposed feedback in your
role as instructor.

## Engineering: a low-voltage indicator circuit

Choose the **Engineering System** through **Help me choose**. Engineering
Companion helps you connect a design question with its parts, drawings and
checks. Reading this example does not require hardware.

Copy the [Engineering inputs](engineering/inputs). Start
with the project map, circuit and parts, and proposed build checks.
Read the schematic and its linked manufacturer source.

> Do these drawings, parts and tests agree?

Trace the connections in the drawing and discuss any mismatch the assistant
finds. Manufacturer specifications, calculated estimates and physical
measurements should remain distinguishable. No circuit was assembled, powered
or physically tested for this example.

Compare your answer with the [illustrative design review](engineering/illustrative_outputs/illustrative_design_review.md)
and [unmeasured test record](engineering/illustrative_outputs/illustrative_test_record.csv).
Physical result fields stay unmeasured until someone performs and records the checks.

Next, change one requirement, such as supply voltage, and ask which calculations
and checks must change. A later physical build needs the specified equipment
and an independently checked build plan.

## PhD and Career: materials-science milestones

Choose the **PhD and Career System** through **Help me choose**. PhD Companion
helps reconcile program requirements, documented progress and possible next
steps.

Copy the [PhD and Career inputs](phd_career/inputs). Read the project map,
invented handbook, student context and evidence record. A fictional first-year
Materials Science student is completing rotations and proposing a ceramic-tile
drying study. No experimental data, operating procedure or real program policy
is supplied.

> What should this student do next?

Trace one proposed action back to the handbook and supplied records. Classroom
induction is not the practical-clearance record; reported rotation completion
is not a signed record. Check that the preferred Week 8 start remains a target,
while missing approval and evidence remain unresolved.

Compare the response with the [illustrative milestone plan](phd_career/illustrative_outputs/illustrative_milestone_plan.md)
and [20-minute advisor agenda](phd_career/illustrative_outputs/illustrative_advisor_agenda.md).
Useful results identify what can be prepared now and what depends on another
record or decision. They do not authorize laboratory work or establish a
placement.

Next, add an invented clearance record to a practice copy and ask which statuses
change. Check the other project and review requirements separately. For your
own degree, bring the applicable handbook and documented progress, and verify
real requirements with the responsible program.
