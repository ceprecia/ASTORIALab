# Watering amount and seedling height change: a synthetic example

This hand-authored report illustrates a bounded revision from the supplied fictional records. It is not the output of a completed assistant session or a physical experiment.

## Question

How did the 14-day height change differ between the two watering groups in this synthetic example?

## Methods

The fictional protocol specified eight pots from one seed batch, with the same stated pot size and potting mix. Four pots received 20 mL of water per day and four received 40 mL per day for 14 days. Each group occupied a separate tray. Height was recorded in centimetres at day 0 and day 14, to one decimal place. Height change was the final height minus the initial height. The descriptive summaries included all eight records, with equal weight given to each pot within its group.

## Results

Mean height change was 3.3 cm in the 20 mL group and 4.1 cm in the 40 mL group, with four pots per group. The 40 mL group therefore had 0.8 cm greater mean height change. Individual changes ranged from 3.0 to 3.4 cm in the 20 mL group and from 4.0 to 4.2 cm in the 40 mL group. These values describe the supplied synthetic records. No statistical test or uncertainty estimate was provided.

## Limitations

Watering amount was confounded with tray position. Random assignment, position rotation and blinded measurement were not documented, and light, temperature and soil moisture were not measured. The numerical difference cannot therefore be attributed to watering amount alone. Height change does not establish plant health or crop yield. The records do not identify an optimal watering amount or support a recommendation for other plants.
