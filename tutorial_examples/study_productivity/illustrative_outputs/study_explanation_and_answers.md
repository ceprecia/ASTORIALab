# Illustrative study explanation and answers

Authored illustrative output for the fictional ECO 101 materials. This is a worked comparison with an assistant's response, not a completed learner session. Every count comes from the [supplied lesson](../inputs/lesson_and_practice.md).

## A short explanation

Count individuals by adding the tallies. Count observed richness by identifying the species with a positive tally. The two quantities answer different questions.

When combining Q1 and Q2, Species A and Species B occur in both samples. Each still counts once toward pooled richness. The pooled record has Species A = 8, B = 7, C = 1 and D = 4. Its total is 20 individuals across four species. Species B contributes 7 / 20 × 100 = 35% of those pooled individuals.

That pooled record covers two quadrats. Habitat B's record covers one. Report the observed counts and sampling effort together; do not turn them into a conclusion about every species present in the whole habitat.

## A practice interaction

An assistant could ask why adding Q1's richness to Q2's richness gives the wrong pooled result. A useful answer names the shared Species A and B and explains that each distinct species is counted once. If the learner adds individuals instead, return to the difference between a species count and an individual count.

This teaching example records no learner response or mastery judgment.

## Self-check

Cover the answer table and explain three distinctions in your own words.

- Individual abundance versus observed species richness.
- A single-sample percentage versus a pooled percentage.
- An observed zero versus evidence about an entire habitat.

Then check the exact definitions and limits in the lesson. If a practice count or sampling condition changes, state it and recalculate before reusing this answer key.
