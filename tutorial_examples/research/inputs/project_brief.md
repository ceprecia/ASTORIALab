# Proofreading break pilot

This project and every participant record are fictional. The data were invented
for learning ASTORIA. No people were recruited and no experiment was conducted.

## Question

In a short proofreading task, does a five-minute quiet break before a passage
change the number of planted typographic errors detected, compared with a
passage preceded by continuous reading?

The question is fixed for this exercise. The prediction that a break helps is
provisional. Do not replace this with a different research question.

## Requested work

1. Turn the brief and protocol into a concise research plan.
2. Check what the supplied observations can and cannot show.
3. Summarize the synthetic numbers descriptively, with their denominators.
4. Leave a short continuation note another assistant can use.

Use only this packet. A literature search, human recruitment, statistical
significance claim and publication are outside this exercise.

## Files

- [Protocol](protocol.md): the proposed design and its unresolved issues.
- [Synthetic results](synthetic_results.csv): eight paired records.

## Completion

A useful result separates the fixed question, provisional prediction, design
limitations and invented observations. It identifies the next design decision
without treating these numbers as empirical evidence that breaks help.
