## Learning outcomes

By the end of this fictional course, a learner should be able to state a procedure's starting conditions, put actions in an executable order, express a decision rule without losing its priority, and define an observable completion check.

## Course sequence

| Week | Topic | Main practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, March 3 to 7 | Readers, goals and starting conditions | Identify missing inputs |
| 2, March 10 to 14 | Action order and decision priority | Write and trace a parcel procedure |
| 3, March 17 to 21 | Testing and revision | Repair a procedure from a failed case |
| 4, March 24 to 28 | Explaining and defending a revision | Present a short demonstration |

The tutorial supplies the Week 2 content. Its March 11 meeting lasts 50 minutes. The instructor may adapt examples while retaining the stated routing rules and assessment criteria.

The Week 2 studio activity uses the eight-point rubric in the [activity file](activity_rubric_and_student_work.md). Its three parcel cases test whether the written procedure can be followed under the supplied conditions.

## Teaching and feedback policies

- Use the approved local sources to judge the exercise. An outside procedure cannot replace this activity's routing rules.
- Support a score with the student's exact wording and the applicable rubric anchor.
- Feedback identifies a strength, a consequential gap and a concrete revision to try.
- The instructor decides grades and individual exceptions. An assistant may draft feedback and a proposed rubric score for instructor review.
- The instructor key and worked scoring example are preparation resources. When preparing a student handout, select the student activity section and keep staff answers in the instructor's notes.
- All dates use UTC. A due time is the submission deadline; this activity has no timed attempt window.
