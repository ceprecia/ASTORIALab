# ABCD facts that control the review (abcd-skill)

Read this reference when the skill or the user asks for it.

## ABCD facts that control the review

Use these as dated orientation and recheck them at invocation:

- ABCD is a United States multisite longitudinal observational cohort, not an
  intervention and not the Healthy Brain and Child Development Study.
- The official study design reports 11,880 children recruited at ages 9 to 10
  across 21 research sites, with repeated non-imaging and imaging follow-up
  over ten years. Recruitment count is not a guarantee of complete data for
  every release, event, or modality.
- Official Release 7.0 was posted 2026-05-13 from a 2025-08-01 data freeze. It
  reports 11,860 participants and 16 events.
- Release 7.0 documentation describes data as complete through five-year
  follow-up, nearly complete at six years, and incomplete for seven-year and
  7.5-year events because collection and assent were ongoing at the freeze.
- Current new access is through the NIH Brain Development Cohorts Data Hub,
  not a new NIMH Data Archive request. Legacy NDA access is limited to
  previously approved users and older release state until their existing
  agreements expire.
- The current controlled-access process includes institutional eligibility,
  an active Federalwide Assurance, an institutional signing official,
  responsible-data training, a security attestation, a Data Use Certification,
  and time-limited renewal. Exact requirements must be rechecked.
- A separate agreement applies to specified dissemination involving separately
  analyzed American Indian or Alaska Native data.
- Data dictionaries can be explored through current official discovery tools
  before participant-level access. Participant-level data remain controlled.
- Participants can contribute repeated events. Family and birth-group
  identifiers encode siblings, twins, or triplets. Rows are not universally
  independent.
- Site, scanner, scanner software, family, repeated observations, and substudy
  structures are distinct dependencies. Standardized acquisition does not
  remove scanner effects.
- Structural MRI, diffusion MRI, resting-state and task fMRI, behavioral and
  neurocognitive assessments, biospecimens, genetics, wearables, linked
  context, and substudies exist in the broader resource, but availability
  varies by participant, event, domain, and release.
- The ABCD-BIDS Community Collection is a derivative layer, not the raw ABCD
  resource. Its version and provenance must be kept separate.

Do not treat an event label as proof that every variable or modality was
collected at that event.
