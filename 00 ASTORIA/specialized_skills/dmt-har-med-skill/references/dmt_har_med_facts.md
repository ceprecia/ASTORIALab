## DMT-HAR-MED facts that control the review

Use these as dated orientation and recheck them at invocation:

- The verified release is OpenNeuro `ds006644` snapshot `1.0.1`, DOI
  `10.18112/openneuro.ds006644.v1.0.1`, released 2025-09-16.
- Snapshot 1.0.1 reports BIDS version 1.10.1 and differs from 1.0.0 by README
  DOI updates.
- The released participant table contains 40 healthy experienced meditation
  practitioners: 20 assigned DMT plus harmine and 20 assigned placebo.
- The primary imaging paper analyzed 39 participants after excluding one for
  excessive motion. Forty released records and 39 analyzed participants are
  different grains.
- All participants attended a three-day meditation retreat. Drug or placebo
  was administered on the second retreat day.
- Resting-state fMRI was acquired before and after the retreat. The post scan
  occurred about two days after drug administration and represents a
  subacute state, not an acute drug scan.
- T1-weighted structural MRI is present only at the post-retreat session.
- Resting-state acquisition used eyes-closed instructions at one Zurich site
  on a 3 T Philips Achieva scanner with a 32-channel head coil.
- Raw cardiac, respiratory, and scanner-trigger physiology is released for
  both sessions.
- The release includes fMRIPrep 23.0.2 outputs and physIO derivative outputs.
  Included derivatives remain products with their own provenance, not
  automatically preferred analysis inputs.
- No diffusion MRI, task fMRI, event files, questionnaire files, behavioral
  phenotype directory, or released psychometric scores were found in
  snapshot 1.0.1.
- The trial collected psychometric and experiential measures, but study
  collection does not establish release availability.
- The machine-readable `dataset_description.json` says CC0. The same
  snapshot README says CC-BY-4.0 and requests citation of the dataset and
  paper. Current OpenNeuro publication documentation also describes public
  datasets as CC0. This remains an official-source conflict for this skill.
- The design lacks both an intervention-free control group and a
  meditation-free drug group.
- More than 80 percent of participants correctly guessed their allocation,
  limiting blinding.
- The mostly white, experienced-meditator sample limits generalization to
  other populations.

Do not call this an acute DMT dataset, a DMT-only intervention, a
meditation-free drug comparison, or a released psychometric dataset.
