# Design facts that control the review (neuropsych-battery-selector)

Read this reference when the skill or the user asks for it.

## Design facts that control the review

### Construct, score, and intended use

- The user chooses what to measure before this skill compares how to measure it.
  Package or test availability never defines the construct.
- Validity attaches to a proposed score interpretation and use, not to a test
  in the abstract.
- Reliability and measurement error must match the planned score. Evidence for
  a total score does not transfer to a subscale, difference, discrepancy,
  composite, change score, or classifier.
- Group raw-score research may not require individual norms, but it still
  requires score-level validity, reliability, fairness, and measurement-error
  evidence for the intended analysis.
- Diagnostic sensitivity or clinical discrimination evidence does not
  establish suitability for change, individual differences, covariate
  adjustment, mechanistic inference, or population screening.

### Population, language, culture, and fairness

- Translation is not adaptation. Verify permission, construct, method, and item
  equivalence, target-population evidence, comparability, and documentation.
- Evidence and norms do not transfer automatically across editions, forms,
  languages, cultures, education and literacy contexts, modes, devices, ages,
  conditions, or settings.
- If norms matter, verify edition, sample, date, variables, representativeness,
  and intended use. Never calculate or interpret norms here.
- Do not use race as a biological proxy or automatic score correction.
- Accommodations can improve access while changing standardization,
  measurement, or rights. Record the tradeoff and require authorization and
  validation rather than silently substituting a form.

### Repeated measures, mode, and version

- Repeated assessment requires exact evidence about interval, prior exposure,
  practice, retest error, responsiveness, form equivalence, order, floor,
  ceiling, and stable mode, device, software, and scoring.
- Alternate forms may reduce item memory but do not erase learning, strategy,
  familiarity, or nonequivalence.
- Digital, remote, paper, and in-person forms are not automatically equivalent.
  Record hardware, software, version, display, input, timing, privacy,
  security, qualification, and export differences.
- Newer is not automatically better and older is not automatically obsolete.
  Lock the exact version selected and do not migrate mid-study silently.

### Battery composition and burden

- Domain labels do not prove that two scores are redundant or complementary.
  Compare observable demands, score meaning, method variance, and incremental
  information.
- Multiple indicators may improve coverage but can also increase shared-method
  variance, multiplicity, cost, fatigue, missingness, and interpretation risk.
- Burden includes time, fatigue, distress, sensory, motor, language and
  communication access, equipment, staff, training, scoring, data handling,
  cost, license, scheduling, and participant acceptability.
- A short battery is not automatically efficient, and a comprehensive battery
  is not automatically valid.
