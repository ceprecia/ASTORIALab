# Research CV Builder

Scientific research career-document guidance grounded in evidence, field conventions, and the reader's decision.

## Purpose

Skill summary:

- **Name:** Research CV Builder
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Focus:** Scientific research careers across graduate training, postdoctoral work, research staff roles, faculty searches, government, nonprofits, and research-focused industry.
- **Core modes:** Critique, Archival CV and Evidence Ledger, Main Research CV, Scientific Research Resume, and Application Tailoring.
- **Optional output:** An original blank research CV template based on the recipient-selected application, format and relevant examples, created only when requested.
- **Guidance basis:** Date-stamped Harvard, UCLA, UCSF, and cross-university career-service guidance integrated throughout this skill.
- **Criteria reviewed:** 2026-07-20 for all listed sources.

The sources below are the guidance basis used to author and audit this skill. This is not a claim that an AI model was trained on them.

Use this skill for scientific research career documents, including:

- PhD students and candidates
- Postdoctoral scholars and fellows
- Research assistants, associates, coordinators, lab managers, and staff scientists
- Research and teaching faculty candidates
- Scientific fellowship, grant, and training applications that require a CV
- Biomedical, government, nonprofit, clinical-research, and industry research roles

This skill is not the primary workflow for generic corporate resumes, non-research professional resumes, artistic CVs, or clinical credentialing documents. If a broad `resume builder` request is not clearly research-related, state the boundary and ask what scientific or research role the document should support. Do not force a research-CV workflow onto a non-research application.

Coordinate with cover letters, research statements, and teaching statements only enough to prevent contradiction or duplication. Do not turn this skill into a general application-writing system.

## Steps

If the user names a mode or gives a clear task, begin directly in that mode. Use the response form selected for the task or the applicable verified reporting policy for any orientation. When an orientation is requested or needed, identify Research CV Builder v1.0 and the selected mode in that form.

If the user names only the skill or leaves the mode unresolved, clarify which mode they want before asking for mode-specific files. Use the selected or configured form for this clarification. When neither specifies a form, a short menu can help. Research CV Builder v1.0 evaluates and builds scientific research CVs and resumes in these modes:

1. Critique an existing document
2. Build or update an archival CV and evidence ledger
3. Build or update a main research CV
4. Build a concise scientific research resume
5. Tailor a document to an opportunity

When relevant, offer to compare suitable formats or create an original blank template for the selected application. Do not present or distribute the Harvard sample itself.

### 1. Route the request

Identify the requested mode, scientific field, career stage, and document purpose. Clarify an unresolved mode using the selected or configured form or the fallback menu above. If the request is outside scientific research, state the boundary before proceeding.

### 2. Gather context in short clusters

Do not send a wall of questions. Ask only what cannot be learned from the named files or opportunity.

**Cluster 1 - Decision context**

- Which mode is requested?
- What role, opportunity, institution type, country, and audience will use the document?
- Are there application instructions, a page limit, required sections, or a deadline?

**Cluster 2 - Evidence context**

- Which CV, resume, evidence ledger, source documents, profile pages, or application materials are authoritative?
- Which facts are verified, user-confirmed, historical, confidential, or uncertain?
- Is the document private, outward-facing, or both?

**Cluster 3 - Output context**

- Is there a trusted visual template or formatting preference?
- Does the user want critique only, a revision plan, or file creation after review?
- Where should approved artifacts be saved?

### 3. Inspect before advising

- Read the current document, evidence ledger, named opportunity, and relevant source files before recommending changes.
- Identify the authoritative version rather than assuming the newest filename is correct.
- Preserve source files unchanged and record hashes when provenance matters.
- If current requirements or field conventions may have changed, verify them through official sources.
- Follow `me.md` and `00 ASTORIA/agent_notes/layout.md` for local privacy and path boundaries.

### 4. Read back the strategy

Before a full critique or build, summarize:

- Mode and intended artifact
- Career stage, scientific field, and target reader
- The three intended first-page messages
- Authoritative evidence sources
- Public and confidential boundaries
- Formatting and application constraints

Ask whether the read-back is correct. Integrate corrections before continuing. This is a strategy gate, not permission to edit files.

### 5. Evaluate with the scientific CV rubric

Use only these assessments:

- `meets`
- `partial`
- `missing`
- `needs verification`
- `not applicable`

| Criterion | Questions | Guidance basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence integrity | Are titles, dates, roles, metrics, statuses, skills, and funding claims supported? | Skill synthesis informed by publication-status, role, and contribution guidance |
| Reader fit | Does the document answer why this scientist fits this role, laboratory, program, or institution? | Harvard, UCSF, Oxford |
| First-page narrative | Are career stage, field, scientific identity, and strongest evidence clear in a rapid scan? | Harvard, Berkeley, UCSF |
| Research trajectory | Do the projects form a credible scientific direction without forcing unrelated work into one theory? | UCSF plus skill synthesis |
| Contribution and independence | Can the reader distinguish participation, collaboration, leadership, original ideas, and resulting outputs? | UCSF, MIT, Cambridge |
| Scholarship | Are authorship, citations, output types, and publication statuses transparent and easy to scan? | UCLA, Berkeley, MIT, Oxford |
| Funding and recognition | Are formal title, sponsor, date, role, amount, purpose, and competitiveness accurate when used? | UCLA, Cambridge, consensus |
| Teaching and people development | Are responsibility, learner level, format, scale, mentoring, and outcomes clear when relevant? | UCLA, UCSF, Yale, Cambridge |
| Methods and reproducibility | Are methods, technical skills, computing, open-science practices, and products supported by experience? | UCLA and Cambridge guidance; skill synthesis for reproducibility and open-science auditing |
| Service and leadership | Does peer review, committee work, outreach, conference work, or leadership show concrete contribution? | Yale, Cambridge, consensus |
| Design and privacy | Is the document readable, consistent, restrained, functional, and free of unnecessary sensitive data? | Harvard, Berkeley, consensus |
| Maintenance | Can the full record be updated and reliably reused for future tailored documents? | Harvard, MIT, Cambridge |

Apply the UCSF checklists as direct target-role criteria only for biomedical faculty applications comparable to their research-focused or research-and-teaching context. Otherwise, treat publication impact, R01-style fundability, fit, long-term vision, independence, and course-responsibility items as career-stage trajectory questions, not pass-fail thresholds. Never treat the checklists' prestige markers as universal requirements.

### 6. Deliver the critique in the selected form

Use the current task's requested form or the applicable verified reporting policy. Address the evidence and decisions below wherever they apply. Use the numbered list as an optional outline. Ground proposed edits in the completed critique and distinguish them from findings.

1. **Reader's rapid-scan interpretation** - what the document currently communicates.
2. **Strongest evidence** - what should be protected.
3. **Rubric findings** - concise evidence for each applicable assessment.
4. **Must fix** - factual, privacy, status, contradiction, or usability problems.
5. **High-value improvements** - changes likely to improve selection decisions.
6. **Optional refinements** - stylistic or lower-impact improvements.
7. **Needs verification** - facts that cannot be responsibly published yet.
8. **Recommended first-page message and section order.**
9. **Next decision** - what the user should approve, reject, or clarify.

Be candid about weak evidence, crowded sections, unclear ownership, and mismatched positioning. Do not use publication counts, prestige venues, or another person's CV as universal benchmarks.

### 7. Obtain approval before file work

Show the proposed changes, additions, removals, and destination. Do not create or edit a DOCX, XLSX, or other artifact until the user explicitly approves the file work. Critique, planning, read-only inspection, and draft wording may remain in chat.

### 8. Build the approved artifact

Use the artifact rules below. Work from copies, preserve source documents, and keep the evidence ledger synchronized when one exists.

### 9. Verify and hand off

Complete structural, evidence, privacy, and visual checks. Report the final artifacts, remaining `needs verification` items, and any deliberate departures from the rubric or source template.

## Artifact Mode 1 - Archival CV and Evidence Ledger

The archival CV is a comprehensive private record, not an application-specific document.

- Include every supported scientific, academic, teaching, clinical, service, leadership, employment, and historical accomplishment.
- Retain older or currently irrelevant evidence instead of deleting it from the archival system.
- Exclude unresolved claims until verified or label them privately as needing verification.
- Do not collect or retain residential-address history. Use only the professional contact details needed for the requested career document.
- Do not collect confidential manuscript identifiers, editor correspondence, review dates, or unpublished peer-review content. Represent reviewing service with nonconfidential venue, role, and aggregate activity when supplied.
- Use professional contact information without a full street address.
- Do not impose a page target.

Use these default evidence statuses:

- `verified` - supported by an official document, public institutional record, authoritative metadata, or original source.
- `user_confirmed` - supplied directly by the user without a separate source.
- `historical_source` - recovered from an older CV, resume, profile, or record.
- `needs_verification` - incomplete, conflicting, unclear, or unsupported.

Recommend a private evidence ledger by default. Critique-only work does not require one.

**Default sheets**

- `evidence` - one record per degree, role, output, award, talk, presentation, teaching activity, mentoring activity, service contribution, skill, certification, or accomplishment.
- `document_map` - maps evidence records to each CV or resume with include, omit, or de-emphasize status, intended section, rationale, and review date.
- `read_me` - defines status, confidentiality, update, and privacy rules.

**Minimum evidence fields**

Record ID, category, title, organization, project, dates, location, master description, measurable evidence, source, verification status, confidentiality, document use, and notes.

Do not include student names or confidential manuscript identifiers, editor correspondence, review dates, or unpublished peer-review content in the evidence ledger or career documents.

## Artifact Mode 2 - Main Research CV

Derive the research CV from the archival record or other verified evidence.

- Make the scientific identity, career stage, and methods legible on page one.
- Foreground research experience, scholarly output, and funding for research-intensive roles.
- Include teaching, mentoring, leadership, peer review, and service in proportion to the role and career stage.
- Select technical skills that are supported by projects, products, training, or responsibilities.
- Use complete field-appropriate citations and make the applicant's name easy to find.
- Separate output types and statuses when the distinction changes interpretation.
- Include unsubmitted or in-preparation work only when the field, target, and application norms support it. UCLA's 2025 guide advises against listing unsubmitted manuscripts, while other current guidance and official examples allow clearly labeled in-preparation work. Never mix it with published work.
- Use reverse chronology within most sections unless the field requires another convention.
- Omit `needs_verification` claims from outward-facing versions.
- Do not force the CV to an arbitrary length. Treat published ranges as context, not rules: Harvard reports two to five pages as typical for graduate students; UCLA suggests three to five pages for doctoral candidates and allows up to ten for STEM postdoctoral scholars; MIT reports two to four pages for younger scholars and four to seven for more experienced scholars; UCSF describes two to fifteen pages across career stages; Oxford and Stanford do not set a fixed limit.

Common sections include Education, Research Profile or Research Interests, Research Experience, Publications, Manuscripts, Preregistrations, Research Products, Invited Talks, Conference Presentations, Funding and Awards, Teaching, Mentoring, Peer Review, Leadership and Service, Technical Skills, Memberships, Languages, and References. Select and order them rather than using every category automatically.

## Artifact Mode 3 - Scientific Research Resume

Build a targeted one- or two-page resume for research-focused industry, government, nonprofit, laboratory, research-associate, or staff-scientist roles.

- Lead with the capabilities and evidence required by the role.
- Prioritize selected projects, scientific methods, technical contribution, reproducibility, research operations, collaboration, leadership, and measurable outcomes.
- Translate academic titles and project descriptions for the intended reader without erasing necessary scientific precision.
- Include only selected publications, presentations, grants, or teaching when they strengthen fit.
- Use accomplishment bullets, not an exhaustive academic record.
- Keep the full scholarly history in the archival CV and ledger.
- Do not inflate team results into individual ownership.

If the target is not a scientific research role, state that this mode is not the right specialized workflow.

## Artifact Mode 4 - Application Tailoring

Before selecting content:

1. Extract the opportunity's required and preferred qualifications.
2. Identify institution type, research or teaching balance, audience, methods, responsibilities, and required materials.
3. Write the three facts the first page should communicate.
4. Map those facts to verified evidence.
5. Reorder, include, omit, or de-emphasize records without changing the underlying evidence.
6. Check that the CV or resume complements the other application documents rather than duplicating them.

The application instructions control. If they conflict with this skill's default format or length guidance, follow the application and note the departure.

## Harvard Example and Template Policy

Choose a current example or template that fits the actual discipline, career stage, country and application. The listed institutional sources are candidates for comparison, not a preselected biomedical or postdoctoral template. A recipient-supplied suitable template takes priority.

- Do not automatically distribute, reproduce, or present the Harvard sample.
- Do not copy its example language, identities, citations, or fictional content.
- Use it to study typography, hierarchy, contact presentation, alignment, citation formatting, and page economy.
- Confirm the current sample edition before relying on sample names or page numbers.
- Combine its visual principles with UCLA's career-stage and discipline guidance and UCSF's biomedical faculty-selection prompts only when the target role and career stage match.
- Preserve a user-supplied visual template when it remains readable, professional, and compatible with the application.
- Prefer the current 2026 packet over older Harvard guides. Do not redistribute older archived guides or import their outdated home-address examples.

Create an original blank research CV template only when the recipient requests one and the applicable file work is authorized. Use the selected format and application requirements. Apply the construction guidance below as the format supports it. Use the DOCX workflow and checks when DOCX is selected. Build an original template and keep Harvard's PDF as a reference.

**Construction for the selected application**

Follow the recipient-selected template, applicable submission rules and current relevant disciplinary examples. Establish page size, type, margins, heading hierarchy, contact fields and citation format for that context. Use genuine paragraph styles, functional hyperlinks and consistent date alignment; keep headings with their content. Include only supported professional identity and relevant records. Check the resulting document visually and preserve accessibility. Do not infer a default discipline, career stage, page count or personal aesthetic from the example sources.

## Source policy

### How to interpret the sources

- **Current official guidance** informs the stable operating rules.
- **Official but dated material** can supply useful principles while its date remains visible.
- **Illustrative examples** demonstrate choices, not requirements, publication benchmarks, or language to copy.
- **Skill synthesis** resolves sources into a practical workflow and must remain labeled as synthesis.

The evidence-ledger schema and status vocabulary, preregistration handling, reproducibility checks, black-only hyperlink treatment, hanging citation indents, and DOCX/XLSX verification procedures are skill synthesis. They are not institutional requirements from the sources below.

### Maintenance rule

- Recheck official sources before a major application cycle or when the criteria-review date is more than one year old.
- Update the Skill Summary review date, affected operating rules, and References list together.
- Record source changes rather than silently replacing criteria.
- Prefer current official guidance when it conflicts with an older handbook or example.
- When current official sources conflict, treat the difference as field, jurisdiction, career-stage, or target-role variation and follow the user's application context rather than selecting one rule globally.
- If a source is unavailable, disclose that the stored summary may be stale and do not claim current verification.
- Treat public individual CVs as prompts for comparison, never as institutional rules or minimum thresholds.

## Boundaries

Operating standards:

1. **Evidence before presentation.** Preserve what is supported, distinguish uncertainty, and never strengthen a title, contribution, metric, publication status, funding role, or skill beyond the evidence. [Skill synthesis informed by UCLA, Berkeley, MIT, Oxford, and Cambridge guidance]
2. **The opportunity controls.** Explicit application instructions, country conventions, and verified disciplinary norms override general advice. [Harvard, UCLA, Oxford, and Cambridge guidance]
3. **The first page has a job.** Decide what three facts the reader should understand, then place the supporting evidence early. [Harvard, Berkeley, and UCSF guidance]
4. **Career stage changes the standard.** Do not judge a graduate student against a senior faculty record or treat aspirational R1 signals as minimum requirements. [UCLA and UCSF guidance; skill synthesis]
5. **Research claims require contribution language.** Distinguish the scientific goal, the user's role, the methods or scale, and the outcome. Use ownership verbs only when supported. [UCSF, MIT, and Cambridge guidance]
6. **Scholarly statuses are not interchangeable.** Keep published, accepted, in press, preprint, submitted, under review, preregistered, and in preparation work accurately labeled and separated when useful. Whether unsubmitted work belongs on the outward-facing CV depends on the field, target, and application. [UCLA, Berkeley, MIT, and Oxford guidance; skill synthesis for resolving differing practices]
7. **Completeness and selection are different jobs.** Preserve the full record in the archival system and select from it for outward-facing documents. [Harvard, MIT, Cambridge, and cross-university consensus]
8. **Simple design supports scientific evidence.** Use readable type, consistent hierarchy, and restrained emphasis. Use machine-readable construction when building the artifact. Decoration must not compete with evidence. [Harvard and cross-university consensus; skill synthesis for machine-readable construction]
9. **Privacy is part of accuracy.** Keep residential addresses, confidential peer-review details, irrelevant personal information, and unsupported credentials out of outward-facing documents. [Harvard and Berkeley guidance; confidential-record and unsupported-credential handling are skill synthesis]
10. **The user keeps judgment.** Explain tradeoffs, recommend a direction, and obtain approval before creating or editing files. [Skill synthesis]

Edge cases:

- **No existing document:** Build an evidence inventory first. Offer a template matched to the recipient-selected application, then create it only when requested.
- **Resume requested without a research target:** State the skill's scientific scope and clarify the intended research role.
- **Only an old CV is available:** Recover historical evidence without treating every old claim as currently verified.
- **Conflicting records:** Preserve both sources, mark the conflict, and ask the user or seek an authoritative record.
- **No evidence ledger:** Critique normally and recommend a ledger before substantial archival or repeated tailoring work.
- **Application instructions conflict with the template:** Follow the application instructions.
- **Non-US application:** Check country and institution conventions before applying US defaults.
- **Unusual research product:** Explain software, datasets, protocols, patents, preregistrations, or public resources when the reader may not recognize their contribution.
- **Faculty checklist used outside a comparable biomedical faculty application:** Convert it into trajectory questions and do not treat prestige markers as requirements.
- **User wants advice only:** Return the advice in the current task's requested form or under the recipient's existing reporting policy. This mode is read-only.

## Handoffs

State:

- Mode completed
- Artifacts created or reviewed
- Strongest improvement
- Remaining `needs verification` items
- Privacy or confidentiality checks completed
- Source or template departures
- Recommended next decision

Treat every outward-facing document as a reviewable draft until the user approves it.

## Checks

### DOCX

- Use the available professional document workflow.
- Check visible paragraphs and tables, not only package XML.
- Render every page and inspect hierarchy, typography, spacing, page breaks, clipping, overlap, orphaned headings, split citations, and hyperlink appearance.
- Confirm page geometry, margins, font declarations, styles, tabs, indents, bullet definitions, headers, and footers.
- Scan the complete package for residential addresses, confidential review information, unsupported credentials, placeholder text, and other user-named exclusions.
- Confirm package integrity and that protected source-document hashes remain unchanged.

### XLSX

- Use the available professional spreadsheet workflow.
- Verify sheet names, unique record IDs, filters, frozen panes, validation lists, wrapped text, date formatting, conditional status formatting, and confidentiality markings.
- Check for formula errors, clipped content, duplicate mappings, and unintended changes to protected evidence.
- Render and inspect every sheet after changes.

### Evidence synchronization

- Cross-check every outward-facing claim against an eligible evidence record.
- Update document mappings and review dates without duplicating records.
- Confirm confidential fields remain private.
- List unresolved verification items in the handoff rather than guessing.
