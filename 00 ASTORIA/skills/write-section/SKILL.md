# Write Section

Develop one bounded section whose content does not fit a dedicated section writer.

## Purpose

Draft or revise a custom section from its communicative purpose, verified
inputs and applicable template/requirements. This is the fallback for such
objects as candidate or training narratives, a proposal's Specific Aims,
resource descriptions, rationales and other explicitly named sections.
It is not a whole-application writer or a replacement for a specialist writer.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md)
completely. Use the [cross-system handoff](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md)
when domain-specialist material or requirements are involved.

Read the relevant MOC by the default in `me.md`, then the sections needed for
this assignment. Read any recipient-supplied writing guidance identified in the brief. If none is supplied, use the document-purpose and evidence requirements and the actual task sources. Writing preferences are not evidence for the project's scientific claims.

Recover the current writing brief: exact document/version, audience, section
purpose, central argument and its provisional or adopted status, any recipient-selected organizational requirements,
source locators, protected terminology, claim ceiling, required structure and
length, and authorized edits. Apply only a writing profile selected for this task or in the recipient's `me.md`. The available references include `writing-style-positive-scientific` and `writing-style-negative-ai`. Apply each independently when selected. Ask only for missing information that changes the
requested wording; continue unaffected work without inventing an answer.

Obtain the exact section and its purpose, required questions/headings, evidence
or experience record, template/example, length and contributor ownership.
For an application, use current requirements from their owner. For a technical
section, use the actual domain-specialist packet. Separate supplied facts from
proposed arrangements, goals, hypotheses and unconfirmed commitments.

## Steps

1. Check that no dedicated writer matches the object. Titles, abstracts,
   introductions, Methods, Results, Discussions, Conclusions and figure/table
   text use their specific skills. A CV/biosketch, protocol, artifact layout
   or transcript also retains its existing capability owner. Do not stretch
   this fallback merely because several objects need prose.
2. Define the section's job in one sentence and its link to the document's
   central argument. Identify the exact reader questions it must answer and
   which content must be supplied by the user/domain lead or contributor.
3. Read the governing template and current requirements. Distinguish mandatory
   structure from the style of an example. Map each required item to available
   source facts or an unresolved input; do not copy an example's claims,
   achievements, resources or institutional arrangements into this project.
4. Follow the governing document, form or recipient-selected structure. Use an outline only when it helps the requested section. Choose organization for the passage's function, preserving required information and evidence boundaries without imposing a common paragraph schema.
5. Draft from the supplied evidence. Replace broad praise or importance claims
   with relevant facts and an explicit explanation of their bearing on the
   section's purpose. Preserve exact terminology, dates, quantities and status.
   A smooth sentence is not a substitute for a missing activity, method or outcome.
6. In candidate narratives, connect documented experience to preparedness,
   development needs and the proposed next step without inventing achievements.
   In training narratives, link each supplied need to an actual proposed activity,
   contributor, timing and progress evidence. Identify unconfirmed arrangements
   as proposed; do not make another person's commitment.
7. In aims or other proposal arguments, distinguish the supported gap,
   objective, hypothesis, planned test and expected contribution. Ask for a
   missing substantive choice or use a clearly provisional scaffold when
   requested. Do not invent a settled central hypothesis, sample or procedure.
8. In descriptions of resources, feasibility or support, trace access and
   capabilities to actual records/owner input. Distinguish available, planned,
   requested and confirmed. Do not treat a public facility description as
   proof of the user's access or a draft letter as a signed commitment.
9. Check the section against each required question and the current argument.
   Repair irrelevant background, repetition, unsupported bridges and vague
   model/analysis language. Route missing technical facts to their owner;
   route policy questions to the requirements owner rather than decide them.
10. Read for fidelity and useful specificity. Apply the requested voice and
    applicable profiles without obscuring limitations or recasting the science.
    Do not expand into other sections or create a parallel application hub.

## Outputs

Return the requested section, with brief consequential questions or source gaps.
If a scaffold was requested, distinguish its provisional choices and empty inputs
from established facts. Keep outputs in the authorized location and maintain
one owner for the canonical document. Do not append a generic compliance dossier.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent experience, expertise, collaborations, institutional support,
  funding, access, budgets, activities, ethics approval or scientific evidence.
- Do not select the opportunity, scientific direction, training commitments or
  institutional representations on behalf of their owners.
- Do not replace official instructions with a successful example's old format.
- A section may be drafted while independent requirements remain unresolved;
  do not certify completion or compliance beyond the supplied evidence.

## Handoffs

Return to the invoking writing owner. The requirements and structure owners
supply application/template constraints; relevant domain owners supply technical
facts and scientific decisions through the shared handoff. Evidence/reasoning,
argument reasoning, substance and citation review remain separate jobs. The integration editor
owns assembling the final application or other document.

## Checks

- Is the assignment truly one bounded custom section?
- Does each required question have a specific supported answer or explicit gap?
- Are material assertions and inferences supported, appropriately qualified and relevant to the selected section purpose?
- Are proposed and confirmed facts, achievements and arrangements distinguishable?
- Did any example wording import facts or commitments into this user's document?
- Does the draft preserve the exact current scientific and institutional scope?
