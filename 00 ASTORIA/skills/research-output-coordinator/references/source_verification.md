# Current primary-source verification

Read this contract before a Part 5 skill makes a requirement-dependent recommendation. Reuse a complete read within the current task. This is a verification method, not a stored set of publisher, repository or institutional rules.

## Establish the exact question

Identify the publication or research output, destination, article/material type, operational stage, institution/funder when applicable, intended access and the action being considered. A rule for a revision, public dataset or particular journal must not silently become a rule for initial submissions, restricted data or every publisher.

Separate a scientific choice from an external requirement. The user owns scientific interpretations, comparisons, tolerances and decisions; an official policy may constrain a destination or action without choosing the science.

## Verify the controlling source

1. Use search to discover candidates, then follow current official navigation to the exact journal, publisher, repository, funder, standard or institutional source. Inspect the relevant full policy, author instructions, official documentation, notice, agreement or PDF section.
2. Check that the page concerns the selected target and stage. Inspect linked amendments, service announcements, current versions, archived status and explicit supersession. A directory entry, university guide, example article, old template, cached summary or search snippet is a lead rather than proof of a destination's current requirements.
3. Treat browser names, recent retrieval, search ranking, cache headers and copyright footers as insufficient evidence of policy currency. If the available retrieval appears incomplete or stale, follow the owner's current policy index or an official alternate format. A different browser alone does not resolve the question.
4. Separate the source's publication/update date, effective date or version, and the date checked. Record only dates actually supplied. A current official page can be undated; identify that limitation and the navigation/version evidence used rather than inventing a date.
5. Resolve apparent conflicts through explicit applicability and supersession. A later applicable notice can change an older general page; a newer unrelated page does not override a controlling requirement. University advice does not establish another publisher's policy. Grant-specific authority remains governed by research-grant-advisor's application contract.
6. If access fails, try a current official alternate page, linked document or available authorized connection. If the controlling content remains inaccessible or conflicting, report that state and continue unaffected work. Do not fill the gap from remembered rules or a third-party summary.
7. Recheck when the destination, article/material type, stage, editorial decision, release date or applicable notice changes, and before a consequential use of a prepared package. Routine edits may reuse unaffected checks within the current working task. Do not claim a stored check remains current indefinitely.

## Return a compact source state

Use only fields needed by the question, with one row per consequential requirement:

| Requirement or question | Exact target/stage | Primary URL and section | Update date / effective version | Checked date | Applicability and status | Finding or unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Statuses are **verified for this task**, **uncertain**, **conflicting**, **inaccessible**, **superseded**, or **not applicable**. Explain not applicable. For an undated source, say so alongside its status. Link the exact supporting policy rather than an organization homepage when the claim is specific.

Distinguish official requirements, advisory recommendations, observed facts and the agent's proposals. A confirmation needed from an author, technical owner or institution stays visibly unconfirmed. Report checks in the current task's requested form or under the recipient's existing reporting policy. A separate saved report requires authorization. Do not automatically create additional audit files. Do not create a second project hub or an automatic source log.

## Preserve separate kinds of evidence

- A complete reporting checklist does not establish methodological validity.
- Successful execution does not establish that the specified results were reproduced.
- Dataset-format validation, uploaded files, a DOI and repository preservation do not establish computational reproducibility.
- Record the original data, code, configurations and environment used for reproduction. Check current sharing requirements without silently upgrading the historical analysis environment.
- The expected outputs and comparison criteria come from the user-selected analysis or technical owner. Do not choose scientific tolerances or exclusions to turn a discrepancy into a pass.
- Keep manual steps visible. Do not replace an established manual workflow or change a pipeline merely to satisfy a preferred packaging convention.

## Shared return and handoffs

Return the bounded target and inspected versions; actual result or artifact; source state where requirements matter; execution/comparison or record evidence; uninspected scope; and the smallest useful next action and owner. Preserve the invoking session and return owner. Read the current research contract when scientific work is in scope.

Borrow the installed shared writing/reporting skills and technical owners for their own jobs. Carry the current scientific claim and its provisional status, selected terminology, evidence, unknowns, authorization and exact output through a handoff. Do not restart a full intake or silently adopt a provisional scientific recommendation.

Preparation, verification, technical execution, reviewed quality control, submission, deposit and public release are separate states. Follow `me.md` for saving and external actions. A request to build these skills does not authorize operating a publisher portal, uploading research or contacting contributors.

Discover available browsing, repository, document and filesystem tools as needed. Use an available service connector when it provides the required capability, or the best authorized fallback. Missing runtime, inaccessible primary content and missing user decisions are different limitations; report the affected scope precisely.
