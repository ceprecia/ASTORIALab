# Write Abstract

Make the work understandable on its own without concealing what remains unknown.

## Purpose

Draft or revise a research abstract, proposal summary, or comparable bounded
summary. Distill the current document rather than a remembered earlier plan.
Represent the whole work in proportion, including a training or application
purpose when that is part of the supplied requirements.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md)
completely. Use the [cross-system handoff](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md)
when domain-specialist material or requirements are involved.

Read the relevant MOC by the default in `me.md`, then the sections needed for
this assignment. Read any recipient-supplied writing guidance identified in the brief. If none is supplied, use the document-purpose and evidence requirements and the actual task sources. Writing preferences are not evidence for the project's scientific claims.

Recover the current writing brief: exact document/version, audience, section
purpose, central argument and its provisional or adopted status, any recipient-selected organizational requirements,
source locators, protected terminology, claim ceiling, required structure and
length, and authorized edits. Apply only a writing profile selected for this task or in the recipient's `me.md`. The available references include `writing-style-positive-scientific` and `writing-style-negative-ai`. Apply each independently when selected. Ask only for missing information that changes the
requested wording; continue unaffected work without inventing an answer.

Use the current full document or verified section summaries, actual result
outputs when reporting completed work, required headings, word/line limits,
and any restriction on citations or abbreviations. Obtain the study status
and the exact contribution to foreground; do not infer results from aims.

## Steps

1. Extract the actual question, gap, objective, design, essential findings or
   planned tests, and justified implication. Mark contradictions between
   sections for the relevant owner. A manuscript and a proposal need different
   tense and different evidence: planned success is not a finding.
2. Set the reader's entry point. Give enough context to make the specific gap
   and purpose understandable without a field history or unearned broad claim.
3. Build a compact functional outline using the required headings. Otherwise
   connect context and gap to the objective, essential approach, key supported
   findings, and bounded consequence. Adapt rather than mechanically allocating
   one sentence to each function.
4. Draft the summary in the required format and selected organization. Make the question, supported findings and limits understandable within the available space. Do not impose a paragraph framework or replace the scientific question with a list of techniques.
5. Include the supplied population/system and essential design information.
   For completed work, preserve the decisive comparisons, estimates and
   uncertainty needed to assess the conclusion. Do not selectively omit a
   material negative or conflicting outcome to improve the story.
6. For proposed work, describe the proposed tests and training/other components
   required by the brief. Distinguish hypotheses and expected outcomes from
   demonstrated results. Missing design decisions stay unresolved.
7. Compare every sentence against the current source document. Check terms,
   numbers, direction, status and scope. The final implication must follow from
   the evidence actually represented, not merely repeat that the topic matters.
8. Compress redundant setup, rare abbreviations and unnecessary study detail.
   Check the real word or line limit and required format. Do not improve a
   readability score by deleting information necessary for accurate interpretation.

## Outputs

Return a complete abstract or revised abstract with only material unresolved
inputs. A requested provisional scaffold must be clearly identified as such.
Do not invent findings or silently complete missing procedures. State a verified
length only when it has actually been counted. Keep the authorized output owner.

## Boundaries

- Do not turn association into causation or a proposed benefit into a result.
- Do not introduce a scientific claim absent from the body.
- Do not add citations, headings or keywords where the supplied venue forbids them.
- Missing results can support a proposal abstract or requested scaffold, not a
  fabricated completed-study abstract.

## Handoffs

Return the summary to the invoking writing owner. Resolve factual conflicts
with section/domain owners; the integration editor reconciles the final body
and abstract. The requirements owner supplies current limits and application
rules; bibliography verification and independent substance review stay separate.

## Checks

- Can a reader identify the question, approach, status and earned takeaway?
- Do the claims, quantities and terms agree with the current body?
- Are major findings or required proposal components represented proportionally?
- Does the last sentence say what follows, within the evidence boundary?
- Does the text meet the actual supplied format and length?
