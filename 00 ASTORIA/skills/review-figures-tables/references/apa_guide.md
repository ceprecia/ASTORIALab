# APA Figure and Table Guide

Use APA 7 as the working basis when no other display style is specified.
Apply the selected venue or assignment instructions to their actual target and
stage. Separate documented requirements from optional readability suggestions.
Official samples illustrate choices; they are not one compulsory design.

## Numbers, titles and notes

Place a bold figure or table number above a brief italic Title Case title,
one double-spaced line below the number. Number figures and tables separately
in order of first mention. Put necessary explanatory notes below. Keep number,
title and notes outside the data image/body rather than crowding the plot.
See APA's [figure](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures/figures)
and [table setup](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures/tables).

Italicize the word *Note* at the start of a general note. Use notes to explain
needed abbreviations, units, sample/count variation, intervals, statistical
symbols and corrections. Include attribution when the display requires it.
Avoid duplicating information already clear in the display. General, specific
and probability notes have different purposes; use only those needed. APA's
[student-paper guidance](https://www.apa.org/ed/precollege/psn/2020/09/apa-style-student-papers)
explicitly describes note typography and paper placement; do not apply its
student-only instructions to a journal submission.

## Figures

Label axes, units and elements clearly. A legend explains symbols and, when
present, should be inside the figure borders. Use sans serif image lettering
at 8–14 pt. Table text generally follows the paper's font. Assess the placed
display at its actual size: high dpi or large source lettering does not prove
legibility after reduction. See APA's [font guidance](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/paper-format/font).

Use adequate contrast and identify categories through more than color alone
where needed, such as labels, shapes or line styles. See APA's
[accessible color guidance](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures/colors).

As design recommendations, share scales across comparable panels, remove
repeated labels and shorten panel titles. Separate declared scales can be
appropriate for different quantities or participant/group levels. Preserve
missingness and measured values. Check that each cell agrees with its row and column definitions.
Use restrained panel letters; keep algebra and longer definitions in the Note
when the plot becomes hard to read. Avoid a redundant second encoding unless
it helps the requested comparison.

## Tables

Provide a heading for every column, including the stub. Center headings and
use sentence case. Left-align stub entries; generally center other entries,
allowing left alignment when it aids reading. Keep comparable entries adjacent.
Use only necessary horizontal rules; omit vertical rules and cell-by-cell
boxes. Repeat headings on subsequent pages. Put shared units in headings or
notes and identify uncertainty clearly. These points follow APA's table setup.
The [sample tables](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures/sample-tables)
show useful layouts, including confidence interval columns or bracketed limits.

Decimal alignment and restrained shading are design suggestions, not universal
requirements established by the public setup page. Consolidate a repeated
sample size only when it is truly shared; keep varying denominators visible.
Define missing-value symbols and keep missing observations distinct from zero.

## Numbers and statistics

Choose precision that preserves useful information and supports comparison.
Keep it consistent within comparable quantities. APA's decimal recommendations
allow judgment; do not round a small estimate and its interval into false zeros.
Exact *p* values normally use two or three decimals; use *p* < .001 below that
threshold. Statistics that can exceed 1 receive a leading zero, such as standard deviations
and unbounded model coefficients. Pearson *r* and *p* omit that zero. Define
abbreviations such as CI; standard symbols such as *M*, *SD*, *n* and *p* need
no expansion. See the [APA numbers and statistics guide](https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/numbers-statistics-guide.pdf).

Labels and notes must retain the supplied meaning of intervals, adjustments,
sample units and transformations. Numerical agreement belongs with the output
verifier. A near-zero contrast or interval crossing zero does not demonstrate
equivalence or absence of an effect.
