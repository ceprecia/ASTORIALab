# Worked Figure and Table Reviews

These are synthetic examples. Apply the review to the assigned display and
supplied evidence. Separate APA requirements from readability suggestions.

## 1. Clear figure

**Input.** A dot plot of seed emergence is readable at the intended width.
Its number and italic title sit above the image. The note defines the time
unit and the verified interval for the estimated mean.

**Ready.** The final-size rendering and supplied verification support the
presentation. No required revisions.

## 2. Crowded figure

**Input.** A chart of water usage is designed at 10 inches and placed at
5 inches. Its 10-point labels render at 5 points. Site names repeat in the
facet headers and legend.

**Needs revision.** Plot labels are too small at final size. Shorten site
names, remove the redundant legend and size the lettering for the placement.
The figure builder owns the revision.

## 3. Cluttered table

**Input.** A transport time table repeats the unit in every cell, boxes each
entry and gives eight decimal places despite a two-decimal reporting brief.
Its estimate and interval columns already have clear headings.

**Needs revision.** The table body is harder to compare than necessary. Use
only the needed horizontal rules, put shared units in the headings or note
and use the requested precision. Keep differing counts visible. The table
builder owns the revision.

## 4. Comparable scales

**Input.** Two panels compare conductivity at different sites. One vertical
axis begins at zero while the other is truncated, and the shared caption
does not identify the difference. An appendix panel shows calibration error
in a different unit.

**Needs revision.** Readers may misread the site comparison. Use a common
declared scale in those two panels or explain a justified difference. The
calibration panel can keep its distinct scale and unit. The figure builder
owns the revision.

## 5. Small quantities

**Input.** A table gives a calibration variance of 0.00043 L² with a
confidence interval of [0.00012, 0.00071]. All three quantities appear as
0.00 in the placed table.

**Needs revision.** The precision erases the estimated variance and its
uncertainty. Use a common precision that shows useful differences and keep
the source values and unit. The table builder owns the revision.

## 6. Disagreement with the output

**Input.** A caption calls the plotted quantity a percentage of samples.
The supplied verification return identifies it as a count per minute.

**Needs revision.** The caption and output describe different quantities.
Ask the output verifier to confirm the intended source and unit. The caption
writer then corrects the description from the verified definition.

## 7. Missing verification evidence

**Input.** A readable display has no figure number. Its note describes
independent sampling runs and standard errors, but the identified source
cannot be inspected.

**Not fully checked.** The figure builder can add the assigned bold number
now. The invoking owner and output verifier can resolve the unverified
sampling and interval definitions when evidence becomes available.
