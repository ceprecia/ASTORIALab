# Plan Research Engagement

Connect supported findings with defined audiences, purposes and feedback.

## Purpose

Plan or evaluate one dissemination or public/community engagement activity.
Distinguish one-way communication from reciprocal engagement and match
evaluation to the intended benefit. Supply a source-bounded brief to existing
writers and artifact builders while preserving the user's scientific claims.

## Inputs

- Supported findings, source locations, selected claim scope and uncertainties.
- Publication/release stage and actual links or identifiers when available.
- Defined or candidate audiences, purposes, channels and relevant partners.
- Requested mode: plan an activity or evaluate an identified activity.
- Available resources, timing, constraints, actual evaluation evidence and return owner.

## Steps

1. Bound the activity and its purpose. Establish whether the user intends
   to inform, support understanding, invite dialogue, involve participants,
   seek input or another specified outcome. Keep suggested aims distinct
   from aims the user or participating community has selected.
2. Read [current primary-source verification](../research-output-coordinator/references/source_verification.md)
   completely. Apply it to relevant publisher release/embargo conditions,
   institutional guidance, channel requirements and any applicable evaluation
   or participation requirements. Confirm the exact output and stage.
3. Identify the audience's information needs, prior knowledge, access needs
   and relationship to the research using supplied evidence. Mark assumptions;
   avoid treating a broad demographic label as evidence of shared preferences.
4. Define the supported takeaway and necessary context. Preserve uncertainties,
   population limits and the difference between proposed work and findings.
   Identify which scientific decisions or facts need the research owner
   before a public-facing claim can be prepared.
5. Select or compare formats appropriate to the purpose. Dissemination may
   use a summary, talk, media briefing or web output. Reciprocal engagement
   additionally specifies how people can contribute, how their input will
   be considered and what feedback or follow-through they can expect.
6. Prepare a bounded brief for shared writers and relevant artifact builders:
   audience, intended understanding or action, selected message, evidence,
   limitations, format, source links and current release constraints. Reuse
   the original caller; do not start a separate writing team or full intake.
7. Plan a proportionate evaluation before the activity when possible.
   Connect each objective to an indicator, evidence source, collection method,
   timing and responsible owner. Distinguish planned measures from collected
   evidence and stated evaluation criteria from choices still open.
8. Separate exposure from understanding, participation, action and longer-term
   effects. Counts of views or attendance measure reach; a learning or impact
   claim needs evidence suited to that claim. For reciprocal work, include
   whose views were heard and how input influenced the research or decisions.
9. Establish a practical schedule, contributor responsibilities and needed
   confirmations. Keep proposed dates, commitments and release status
   distinguishable from confirmed ones. Route participant-facing research
   procedures or new analyses to their appropriate design/technical owners.
10. In evaluation mode, compare actual evidence with the stated objectives.
    Inspect denominators, missing responses, audience coverage and source
    limitations. Report unexpected outcomes and unresolved explanations;
    do not infer causation or population-wide effects from simple reach data.
11. Return the plan or evaluation, relevant source state, unconfirmed inputs,
    and the next useful action. Include a feedback or follow-through step
    for reciprocal engagement and a handoff for authorized release.

## Outputs

For planning, return the activity objective, audience, message/evidence brief,
channel or format, responsibilities, timing and goal-matched evaluation.
For evaluation, return the actual evidence, objective-by-objective findings,
limitations, lessons and next action. Include source state where rules matter.

Return planning and check results in the current task's requested form or under the recipient's existing reporting policy. A separate saved report requires authorization.
Use the owning project and me.md for any requested artifact; do not create
a parallel engagement hub or automatic monitoring routine.

## Boundaries

- The user retains the scientific interpretation, intended claim and commitments.
- Engagement and dissemination planning do not themselves authorize contact,
  participant recruitment, data collection, public posting or publication.
- A polished public summary does not establish comprehension or impact.
- Do not invent audience feedback, participant consent, quotations, reach
  statistics, endorsements or partnership commitments.
- Evaluation of an activity does not independently validate the research.
- When scientific evidence changes, identify the affected messages and
  outputs without discarding unrelated engagement work.

## Handoffs

- Preserve the original session and return owner.
- Borrow shared writing capabilities for audience-appropriate prose and
  artifact builders for the selected delivery format.
- Use [research-critique](../research-critique/SKILL.md) for
  requested scientific appraisal and existing analysis/design owners for
  new evaluation procedures or analyses.
- Use [maintain-publication-record](../maintain-publication-record/SKILL.md)
  for publication/correction status affecting communication.
- Leave institutional/media correspondence and release with their authorized owners.

## Checks

- The audience and activity have an explicit purpose grounded in current inputs.
- The message can be traced to supported findings and their limitations.
- Format and evaluation match the purpose, including reciprocal input where intended.
- Observed reach, reported understanding and inferred impact are distinguishable.
- Timing, unconfirmed commitments, feedback responsibilities and next owner are clear.
