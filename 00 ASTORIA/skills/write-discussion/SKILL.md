# Write Discussion

Explain what the findings mean for the question and what they leave unresolved.

## Purpose

Draft or revise a Discussion from current findings and a user-owned
interpretation. Develop the contribution at the level established by the findings, preserving the distinction between supported interpretation, uncertainty and speculation. Do not replace interpretation
with a result inventory or a tour of cited studies.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md)
completely. Use the [cross-system handoff](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md)
when domain-specialist material or requirements are involved.

Read the relevant MOC by the default in `me.md`, then the sections needed for
this assignment. Read any recipient-supplied writing guidance identified in the brief. If none is supplied, use the document-purpose and evidence requirements and the actual task sources. Writing preferences are not evidence for the project's scientific claims.

Recover the current writing brief: exact document/version, audience, section
purpose, central argument and its provisional or adopted status, any recipient-selected organizational requirements,
source locators, protected terminology, claim ceiling, required structure and
length, and authorized edits. Apply only a writing profile selected for this task or in the recipient's `me.md`. The available references include `writing-style-positive-scientific` and `writing-style-negative-ai`. Apply each independently when selected. Ask only for missing information that changes the
requested wording; continue unaffected work without inventing an answer.

Read the current Introduction/question, actual Results and relevant methods,
verified prior work, alternatives and material limitations. Recover the intended
study answer, contribution and stakes. A user/domain lead owns substantive
selection. For a requested provisional draft or scaffold, preserve the
provisional contribution explicitly instead of inventing a settled position.

## Steps

1. Extract the research question, supported answer, proposed contribution,
   significance and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish the user's decisions
   from recommendations, reviewer suggestions and unsettled options. If an
   essential interpretive choice is missing, ask that question while continuing
   independent passages. Do not require a separate roadmap approval merely
   because the section is a Discussion.
2. Use the recipient- or venue-selected Discussion structure. Offer a task-sized outline only when it helps the requested work. Keep supported interpretations, alternatives and unresolved choices distinguishable; do not impose one paragraph schema on every interpretive move.
3. Open by giving the supported answer and what it changes or clarifies for
   the larger question. Explain the earned stakes in the space the argument
   needs. Do not substitute the study objective, a repeated Introduction,
   a list of regions/contrasts or a catalog of significant results.
4. Assess whether the findings support the proposed interpretation, including relevant assumptions, alternatives and limits. Explain material inferential steps without imposing a paragraph framework. Include result detail needed for that assessment and preserve complete statistical reporting in Results; stronger wording cannot supply missing support.
5. Compare with relevant prior work by relationship: confirm, extend, refine,
   reconcile, challenge or constrain, as the actual evidence permits. State why
   agreement/disagreement matters and what could account for a difference.
   Distinguish a study using another task, measure, population or inferential
   method from direct support for the current result or proposed mechanism.
6. Address credible alternatives and uncertainty. Separate measured findings,
   statistical inference, interpretation and speculation. Decoding, activation,
   association, temporal order and causation are different claims; do not trade
   one for another or rename a construct for fluency.
7. Connect each material limitation to the contribution it bounds, the larger
   question left open and a targeted extension that could resolve it. Preserve
   the strengths that make the present contribution informative. Do not pad
   a generic limitations inventory or promise that an untested extension will work.
8. Close by returning to the established contribution and its justified
   consequence or useful next question. The Conclusion writer may distill this
   further; do not enlarge the answer to make the closing sound important.
9. Reverse-outline topic sentences and inspect the reasoning. If removing
   author names and detailed statistics destroys the interpretive progression,
   rebuild the main ideas and warrants. Keep necessary quantitative bounds.
   Check that literature relationships and transitions do not introduce new
   unsupported premises.
10. Compare the draft with the current question, Results, methods and accepted
    claim limits. Apply readability/style guidance without removing precision.
    Return substantive conflicts to their owner; repair presentation within
    the existing scope without a new approval ceremony.

## Outputs

Return Discussion prose, or the requested provisional outline/scaffold, with
only material missing evidence or interpretive decisions. Label provisional
status at the relevant scope. Do not convert a draft hypothesis into an
established finding. Preserve the canonical document's assigned owner.

## Boundaries

- The user/domain lead selects the contribution; the writer articulates it.
- Do not introduce major unreported results, untested mechanisms or new study variables.
- A finding being interesting does not establish its cause, scope or practical value.
- Required genre functions and reasoning checks work together; neither licenses
  unsupported content or mechanically fixes paragraph count and sentence order.
- Critique findings remain proposals until adopted by the user/domain owner.

## Handoffs

Return to the invoking writing owner. The evidence owner resolves a missing
literature basis, and the domain owner resolves a scientific interpretation.
Use independent review for material inferences when needed; a named paragraph-method reviewer requires recipient selection. The substance and citation verifiers retain their distinct checks. Pass the established conclusion
and limits to the Conclusion writer through the coordinator.

## Checks

- Does the opening answer the actual question and explain its earned significance?
- Does each major paragraph interpret rather than merely repeat Results?
- Do evidence and warrants support the actual main idea and central argument?
- Are literature relationships, alternatives and claim limits explicit and accurate?
- Do limitations bound this contribution rather than apologize for every omission?
- Does the ending preserve the same contribution and provisional status?
