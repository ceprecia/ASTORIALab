# Develop Writing Evidence

Explain what the evidence lets the writer say, and why.

## Purpose

Own a bounded evidence-to-reasoning plan for a writing unit. Identify which
source details establish, qualify, contrast with or limit its claim. This
prepares content for a writer; it is distinct from bibliography verification,
broad literature synthesis and an independent scientific claim audit.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md).
Use the current brief, relevant MOC and recipient-supplied writing guidance, exact target
claim, section purpose, central argument and its status. Obtain the bounded
source set, source locators and known limits. A paper title or citation string
is not evidence of a finding.

## Steps

Organize the evidence plan for the task and the recipient- or venue-selected structure. Use a named paragraph framework only when the recipient selects it.

1. State the exact proposition the unit needs to support. Identify its
   population, construct, comparison, outcome and inferential strength when
   relevant. Preserve uncertainty and the user's terminology.
2. Read the relevant passages, tables or outputs in the supplied sources.
   Record enough location information to retrieve each detail. Distinguish
   full-text, abstract-only, secondary summary and unavailable access.
3. Extract only the findings or procedural facts the proposition needs.
   Preserve sample, design, measurement, comparison, direction and uncertainty
   when those details determine relevance. Do not substitute a nearby finding.
4. Organize the source findings so the reader can assess what they support or limit. A source may support one clause and bound another. Include disagreement and contextual differences that materially change the inference.
5. When a claim depends on an inference, explain how the observation supports it, which assumptions it needs and what it cannot establish. Separate the source author's interpretation from the writer's proposed interpretation.
6. If a proposed connection to the section or central argument requires an unsupported mechanism, identify the gap. Do not supply the mechanism as persuasive language.
7. Compare plausible interpretations only as needed to diagnose the writing
   problem. Return a genuine scientific dispute to the domain lead rather
   than choosing a preferred interpretation for smoother prose.
8. Return the smallest sufficient content set and exact missing-information
   questions. Recommend a bounded search only when supplied sources cannot
   answer the needed proposition; discovery stays with paper-search.

## Outputs

Return the smallest useful evidence plan in the requested structure. Use a table or annotated outline when it suits the assignment. Include retrievable source locators and material assumptions, limits or gaps. Keep synthesis notes distinct from
publication prose. Missing evidence is a gap, not a fabricated citation.

## Boundaries

- Do not equate a relevant topic with direct support for a proposition.
- Do not turn absence of a reported effect into evidence of no effect.
- Do not average away conflicting constructs, methods or populations.
- Do not give a general judgment that a contested scientific claim is valid;
  research-literacy owns a requested bounded scientific claim audit.
- Profiles apply only to formal prose production as specified in `me.md`.

## Handoffs

Return the evidence plan to the assigned section writer through
writing-coordinator. verify-writing-citations verifies identity and exact
source-to-claim support. Domain questions follow
[cross-system handoffs](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md);
research-literacy or research-critique retains its scientific review scope.

## Checks

- Every factual anchor has a locator and an honest access status.
- The warrant adds reasoning instead of repeating the evidence.
- Counterevidence and qualifications remain attached to the affected claim.
- The proposed link advances the stated argument without increasing its scope.
