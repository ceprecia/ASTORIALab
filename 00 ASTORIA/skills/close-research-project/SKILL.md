# Close Research Project

Make a project's remaining work, preserved materials and future responsibilities explicit.

## Purpose

Prepare closeout, preservation or handover for one bounded research project.
Connect the actual project state to its obligations, authoritative materials,
retention needs, access arrangements and responsible people.

Select the requested outcome: completion review, preservation planning, paused
project handover or active project handover. Transferring responsibility does
not require declaring the project finished. Keep a plan separate from completed
transfers, institutional closeout and verified preservation.

## Inputs

- Requested outcome, project boundary, original invoking owner and current state.
- Owning MOC read by me.md's default, relevant project instructions, actual
  deliverables and the existing action tracker or correspondence records.
- Authoritative data, code, environments, protocols, manuscripts and release versions.
- Known sponsor, institutional, participant-consent and agreement obligations.
- Current ownership, proposed successor/custodian, access and storage arrangements.
- User-selected preservation goals, time constraints and unresolved decisions.

Do not infer completion, ownership or outstanding obligations from folder names,
unchecked tasks or the absence of a document in a single location.

## Steps

1. Read [source verification](../research-output-coordinator/references/source_verification.md)
   completely. Also read [repository candidates](../research-output-coordinator/references/repositories.md)
   when comparing preservation destinations or inspecting deposited outputs.
   Apply the verification contract to all requirement-dependent advice.
2. Establish the requested outcome and work boundary. Identify whether the
   project remains active, is paused or is considered complete by the user.
   Separate that scientific/project decision from an institution's formal closure.
3. Verify applicable obligations from current project-specific primary sources:
   award and sponsor terms, institutional records/data policies, relevant consent
   and agreements, and existing commitments. Mark inaccessible sources and
   unresolved applicability; another university's guidance is not the user's rule.
4. Inspect the bounded project record and authoritative materials. Distinguish
   active drafts, final outputs, raw data, derivatives, code/configurations,
   environments, decision records, publications and deposited versions. Identify
   missing provenance or competing copies without choosing a winner silently.
5. Reconcile outstanding work with actual evidence: analyses or QC still open,
   manuscripts/revisions, reports, deposits, contributor commitments, access
   renewals and institutional actions. Preserve agreed future milestones and
   use the existing tracker rather than creating a parallel task list.
6. Prepare the needed retention and preservation decisions by material category.
   Identify the applicable source, duration or review trigger, custodian, location,
   intended access and unresolved confirmation. Do not import a fixed retention
   period from a generic guide or infer that a publication ends retention duties.
7. Assess preservation needs: readable formats, documentation, dependencies,
   version identification, available integrity evidence, backups and recovery
   evidence. Report what has actually been inspected or tested. Retained files,
   a repository DOI and a reproduction test are separate kinds of evidence.
8. Prepare the requested handover. Identify the recipient/custodian, materials,
   existing locations, account or resource responsibilities, access needed,
   unresolved work and a useful restart point. Distinguish proposed access,
   access granted, recipient access tested and responsibility accepted.
9. Route concrete remaining work to its owner within current authorization:
   scientific interpretation or analysis, release preparation, repository checks,
   documentation, or institutional reports and closure. No automatic transfer,
   deletion, archive move, message or account change follows from the plan.
10. Return observed completion, remaining obligations, preservation/handover
    recommendations, current source state and the smallest next actions with
    their owners. Record an authorized progress update only through its existing
    capture owner; do not mark a project complete merely because this task ended.

## Outputs

Return the compact record needed by the requested outcome:

- Project boundary and actual active, paused or completed status and its basis.
- Authoritative materials and existing locations, with any unresolved copy/version issue.
- Outstanding work, obligations and responsible owners in the existing project context.
- Preservation/retention recommendations tied to applicable sources and confirmations.
- Handover contents, access and responsibility status, and a practical restart point.
- Completed versus proposed actions, uninspected scope and the next action/owner.

Return the plan and verification findings in the current task's requested form or under the recipient's existing reporting policy. A separate saved report requires authorization.
When an artifact or MOC update is authorized, follow me.md, preserve the existing
MOC architecture and use its owning location. Do not create a second project hub,
an automatic closeout dossier or a new log of every preservation check.

## Boundaries

- Follow me.md for saves, moves and external actions. A closeout request does
  not silently authorize cleanup, destruction, public sharing or institutional filing.
- Keep useful scientific variants, original inputs and provenance under their
  project rules. Unlinked or old material is not disposable on that basis alone.
- The user and authorized institution retain decisions about project completion,
  ownership, retention, financial closure and formal obligations.
- A handover plan does not prove a recipient has access or accepted responsibility.
  An active handover must preserve the project's active status and remaining work.
- General guidance supplies planning questions; only applicable primary sources
  and actual confirmations establish the project's requirements.

## Handoffs

- prepare-research-release prepares selected preservation or sharing packages;
  verify-repository-release checks the exact existing record and inspected files.
- analysis-coordinator and the relevant technical owner resolve analysis,
  environment, QC and reproduction work without treating closeout as scientific approval.
- chat-handoff prepares context for an explicitly named AI chat; this skill owns
  the project handover record for a human recipient. capture-progress records
  authorized progress; write-protocol documents established procedures.
- folder-janitor handles an explicitly authorized filing or move task. Shared
  writers and artifact skills prepare requested reports from verified facts.
- Return to the original invoking owner; institutional reports, account transfers
  and formal closure remain with the responsible people or offices.

## Checks

- Requirements have project-specific sources, checked applicability and visible unknowns.
- Completion, preservation, access and acceptance statements have actual evidence.
- Active work, agreed milestones and project provenance remain represented.
- Handover materials identify their custodian, locations and remaining work.
- No plan, completed transfer, archive state or reproduced result is substituted for another.
