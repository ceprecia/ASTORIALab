# Patterns with fixes

These are synthetic teaching examples, not findings from a real project or
records of a validation run. Each pair preserves the stated content while
changing expression. Use only verified task facts in actual writing; never add
numbers, methods or claims merely to replace a vague phrase.

## Filler and intensity words

Before: "The seedlings genuinely grew from 4 cm to 7 cm. Importantly, the control seedlings remained at 4 cm."

After: "The seedlings grew from 4 cm to 7 cm. The control seedlings remained at 4 cm."

The filler adds no measurement or inference.

## Inflated vocabulary

Before: "We utilized a cutting-edge camera to record leaf area daily."

After: "We used a camera to record leaf area daily."

A claimed technical advantage needs its actual specification and relevance,
not an adjective added during editing.

## Copula dodges

Before: "The reference sample serves as the calibration standard."

After: "The reference sample is the calibration standard."

Retain a functional verb when the distinction between a role and an identity
matters technically.

## Signposts and throat-clearing

Before: "It is worth noting that 18 of the 20 samples passed the stated quality criterion."

After: "Eighteen of the 20 samples passed the stated quality criterion."

The opening can be removed without changing the result.

## Manufactured contrast and rhythm

Before: "It is not a retention effect; it is a measurement difference between the two instruments."

After: "The two instruments gave different measurements."

This pair assumes the supplied evidence already excludes a retention effect.
If it does not, the editor must preserve the unresolved scientific question
instead of using style to decide it.

Before: "The samples were heated, the samples were cooled, and the samples were weighed."

After: "The samples were heated, cooled, and weighed."

Preserve separate sentences when the steps have different actors or conditions.

## Hollow analysis

Before: "The sensor agreed with the reference to within 0.2 units, highlighting the enduring importance of this approach."

After: "The sensor agreed with the reference to within 0.2 units."

The measurement does not by itself supply a broader claim about importance.
If the approved material supplies an implication, explain that implication
with its evidence instead of inventing one.

Before: "Experts have long argued that the coating reduces corrosion."

After: identify the source and its relevant result, or omit the claim when
that support is unavailable. A stylistic rewrite cannot supply a missing source.

## Padding

Before: "In this section we will describe the washing procedure. The samples were washed twice with the stated buffer."

After: "The samples were washed twice with the stated buffer."

Keep an orientation sentence when the document's purpose requires it; remove
announcements that repeat information the heading and procedure already give.

## Typography and layout

Before: "The calibration finished — all six reference measurements met the criterion."

After: "The calibration finished; all six reference measurements met the criterion."

Apply the current profile and user preferences to generated punctuation while
preserving quotations, notation and mandated wording.

Before: "Let me know if you have any questions. I hope this helps clarify the method."

After: end with the last useful method statement. Do not invent a meeting,
deadline or next action to replace a sign-off.

## Research writing

Before: "The results may potentially reflect a temperature effect."

After: "The results may reflect a temperature effect."

One calibrated hedge preserves the proposed interpretation and its uncertainty.

Before: "Further research is needed to examine these findings."

After: state the specific unresolved question and a supported follow-up only
when the current scientific frame supplies them. Otherwise flag the gap to
the writing owner rather than designing a study through a style edit.

## Using the checker

The script identifies selected wording and layout patterns. Review each hit
against the actual task, scientific terminology and protected source material;
use an allowed technical phrase when appropriate. Read the whole draft for
repetition, altered meaning and missing context that a pattern matcher cannot
establish. Report only checks actually performed. A clean result is not an
AI-authorship test or a scientific validation.
