# Writing Style: Positive, Scientific

## Purpose

- **Writing context:** Formal scientific prose, including manuscripts, scientific abstracts, grant prose, posters, formal scientific reports, protocols, methods documentation, figure legends, table notes, and comparable research communication.
- **Activation:** Recipient-selected for the current task or explicitly recorded in their own preferences. It is not a default stack for all scientific prose.
- **Role:** Passive reference. The active writing capability produces and revises the prose.
- **Evidence status:** Externally informed and not personally calibrated to the user's individual voice.

This profile provides expression-level patterns and examples. It does not
draft, revise, select content, organize an argument, choose evidence, critique
reasoning, determine an inference, or apply itself.

## Application rules

- The active writing capability owns the prose and decides which profile rules
  fit the approved writing task, document type, audience, and venue.
- Apply these patterns only after the scientific content, argumentative
  sequence, evidence boundary, and inferential limits are established.
- Preserve approved claims, constructs, variables, estimands, methods,
  evidence, citations, statistics, interpretations, organization, and exact
  terminology.
- Change wording, syntax, local sentence flow, tone, punctuation, and
  formatting without changing scientific meaning.
- If a desirable style change would alter a claim, evidence selection,
  inference, interpretation, paragraph function, or section organization, do
  not make that change. Return the issue to the writing capability or the
  research capability that owns the decision.
- Report applied profiles, profile warnings or verification details when called
  for by the current task or the recipient's existing reporting policy.
- Do not activate this profile merely because a research skill is active. It
  remains inactive for literature searching, paper summaries, synthesis notes,
  critique, strategy, data analysis, MOCs, captures, profile building, skill
  building, personal writing, ordinary email, ordinary notes, and
  ordinary chat.
- Examples below illustrate expression only. They do not supply findings,
  interpretations, or claims for another project.

## Scientific style target

Use precise scientific terms, concrete agents and actions, readable sentence
construction, and restrained evidence-proportional language. Make the approved
meaning easier to recover without simplifying away necessary technical detail.

## Terminology and word choice

### Concrete scientific agents, actions, and objects

Name who or what performed an action and what was measured, estimated, or
observed. Avoid empty process nouns and sentences in which the scientific
action has no visible actor.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "An investigation of seed storage was conducted."
- "There was an examination of catalog errors."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "We tested the supplied seed-storage question."
- "We examined catalog errors."

**Boundary:** Do not add an agent when the source does not identify one or when
the measured object is the appropriate grammatical focus.

### Direct verbs instead of unnecessary nominalizations

Use the scientific action as the main verb when a noun plus a generic verb
would say the same thing less directly.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "We performed an evaluation of soil acidity."
- "A comparison of file encodings was conducted."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "We evaluated soil acidity."
- "We compared file encodings."

**Boundary:** Preserve established scientific nouns such as `activation`,
`connectivity`, `adaptation`, and `prediction error` when they name the actual
measure, construct, or phenomenon.

### Precise relationship and effect verbs

When the approved evidence identifies a direction or relation, express it with
the narrowest accurate verb rather than a generic phrase such as `had an
effect`, `was related`, or `changed`.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "Irrigation was related to plant growth."
- "The filter had an effect on throughput."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "Report the observed association between irrigation and growth from the supplied analysis."
- "Name the measured throughput change and the comparison that supports it."

**Boundary:** The writing capability may use `increased`, `reduced`, `caused`,
or another stronger verb only when the approved design and inference license
that wording. Style never upgrades an association into an effect.

### Stable terminology for constructs and measures

Use one approved term for each construct, condition, group, outcome, and
analysis. Scientific repetition is often clearer than ornamental synonym use.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "Alternating among `enzyme activity`, `enzyme amount` and `reaction rate` for one variable."
- "Using `validation set` and `test set` interchangeably."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "Use the source-defined term for each quantity and distinguish amount from rate."
- "Name each held-out dataset consistently with its actual role."

**Boundary:** Vary ordinary nontechnical wording when it improves readability,
but never replace a protected construct or measure with a stylistic
near-synonym.

### Familiar words around necessary technical language

Keep necessary disciplinary terminology and express the surrounding prose in
familiar language. Technical precision does not require inflated general
vocabulary.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The samples subsequently underwent a process of drying."
- "The catalog operationalized an archival retrieval milieu."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "The samples then dried."
- "The catalog helped readers find archived records."

**Boundary:** Do not replace a precise technical term with a familiar but less
accurate word merely to make the sentence sound simpler.

### Precise modifiers and quantifiers

Replace vague degree words with supplied quantities, directions, or bounded
descriptions when they are available.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "Many samples changed substantially."
- "The importer was very fast."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "State the source-supported count and direction of change; retain an explicit gap if they are unavailable."
- "Give the measured throughput, input size and comparison when the source supplies them."

**Boundary:** Never invent a quantity, effect size, threshold, category, or
direction. Retain a qualitative description when the source supports only a
qualitative description.

### Preserve approved modality and evidential strength

Match verbs and qualifiers to the scientific status already established by
the study and its owning research capability.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "Changing `was associated with` to `caused` during a style revision."
- "Changing `may reflect` to `demonstrates` to sound decisive."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "Preserve the association wording supported by the approved design."
- "Keep an interpretation conditional when competing explanations remain."

**Boundary:** This profile preserves approved modality. The user determines whether
the underlying inference is warranted, supported by Research Critique,
Research Companion, or the appropriate scientific capability.

## Sentence construction

### Purposeful active and passive voice

Use active voice when agency or author responsibility matters. Use passive
voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the
procedure or scientific object.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "A comparison of the two catalogs was performed by us."
- "We dried the samples when the process is the paragraph's topic."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "We compared the two catalogs."
- "The samples were dried when the process is the paragraph's topic."

**Boundary:** Do not impose active voice as a universal rule. Follow the
sentence focus, disciplinary convention, and journal requirements.

### Keep the subject and verb close

Place long qualifications where they do not separate the grammatical subject
from its main action.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The extraction procedure, following the storage checks and the recorded operator review, recovered the analyte."
- "The records that passed several checks showed, after the duplicate review, improved consistency."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "The extraction procedure recovered the analyte after the documented checks and operator review."
- "After duplicate review, eligible records were more consistent. State their eligibility criteria separately."

**Boundary:** Keep a qualification beside the term it truly modifies when
moving it would create ambiguity.

### Control clause and information load

Give a sentence one main job or two tightly related jobs. Split independent
findings or qualifications when their combination obscures the main action.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The extraction yielded material, which was stored under the selected conditions, although recovery varied across batches that had different preparation histories."
- "The catalog was searched and revised and archived in a sequence that is unclear."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "The extraction yielded material. State its storage conditions separately and report batch differences with their source-supported preparation context."
- "State the catalog search, revision and archiving operations in their actual order."

**Boundary:** Sentence length is a diagnostic, not a quota. Keep a longer
sentence when its relationships remain easier to understand together.

### Use parallel grammatical structure

Express coordinate comparisons, aims, and procedures in matching grammatical
forms so the reader can see that they have the same role.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The workflow included checking records, label review and operators had to archive outputs."
- "We assessed recovery, processing time and whether the files remained readable."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "The workflow included checking records, reviewing labels and archiving outputs."
- "We assessed recovery, processing time and file readability."

**Boundary:** Do not force parallel wording onto items that have
different logical or methodological roles.

### Use explicit referents

Replace ambiguous pronouns and empty references with the exact approved object
when more than one antecedent is plausible.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The catalog and the archive were compared; this was incomplete."
- "The model used the collection, which needed revision."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "State whether the catalog comparison or the archive inspection was incomplete."
- "Name the model, collection or operation that needed revision."

**Boundary:** Retain a pronoun when its antecedent is immediate and
unambiguous. Repeating every noun can make prose mechanical.

### Keep tense consistent with rhetorical function

Use tense consistently when sentences perform the same function. Distinguish
what the study did from what the current evidence or manuscript states.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "We calibrate the instrument and then recorded its output."
- "The model predicted demand, and this result suggests a seasonal pattern when both refer to completed work."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "We calibrated the instrument and then recorded its output."
- "The model predicted demand, and this result suggested a seasonal pattern."

**Boundary:** Tense conventions vary by section, field, and journal. Use the
venue and the writing capability's approved rhetorical function rather than a
universal tense formula.

## Local flow and cohesion

### Move from established information to new information

Begin a sentence from information the preceding sentence has established, then
place the new or emphasized material where the reader can recognize its role.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The catalog contains scanned maps. Search results were produced from their descriptions."
- "An archive stores checksums. File integrity was the reason for this in the workflow."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "The catalog contains scanned maps. Their descriptions support search."
- "An archive stores checksums. The workflow uses them to check file integrity."

**Boundary:** A direct finding sentence may lead with the new finding when that
is the approved purpose. Old-to-new flow is a reader strategy, not a mandatory
sentence formula.

### Attach statistics to scientific meaning

State the approved scientific comparison or pattern before or alongside its
statistical support. Do not make a bare test label carry the sentence.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "The test was significant, [test statistic and uncertainty]."
- "There was a result for the model."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "Name the supplied comparison and observed direction before its verified estimate and uncertainty."
- "State the evaluated model outcome and its source-supported comparison; leave unavailable values unresolved."

**Boundary:** The writing capability must receive the scientific takeaway and
statistical interpretation from an approved source. The profile does not
derive the finding from a test statistic or decide whether the comparison is
meaningful.

### Remove needless words without removing information

Cut phrases that add no scientific content while retaining details needed for
accuracy, reproducibility, attribution, and qualification.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "In order to compare the records, we conducted an examination of their fields."
- "It is important to note that the manifest names every retained file."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "We compared the records’ fields."
- "The manifest names every retained file."

**Boundary:** Concision never authorizes deleting a method, boundary,
exception, uncertainty statement, or reporting detail required to understand
or reproduce the work.

## Scientific tone and qualification

### Use restrained, evidence-proportional scientific tone

Describe importance through the approved scientific consequence rather than
through promotional adjectives, certainty theater, or unsupported novelty.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- "This groundbreaking catalog solves every retrieval problem."
- "The procedure is revolutionary."

**Prefer patterns like:**

- "State which retrieval task was evaluated and the supported limits of the result."
- "Name the verified procedural difference and its consequence."

**Boundary:** Do not invent the consequence used to replace an empty
importance label. If the approved material does not specify why a finding
matters, the writing capability must request or retain a more limited statement.

## Notation and formatting

### Preserve scientific notation and established compounds

Use notation consistently and distinguish mathematical symbols from decorative
punctuation. Preserve established methodological compounds and rewrite unclear
modifier stacks.

**Avoid patterns like:**

- Writing a negative value as a hyphenated label such as `-3.2-related change`.
- Inventing a stacked label such as `sample-storage-duration-related-extraction efficiency`.

**Prefer patterns like:**

- Preserve mathematical signs and expressions such as `1 - p` and source-defined contrasts exactly as required by the notation and output format.
- Preserve established methodological compounds from the actual sources; rewrite an unclear modifier stack by stating the relationship between its quantities.

**Boundary:** Preserve journal-required notation, table dashes, identifiers,
software names, code, command flags, filenames, citations, and mandated wording.
Choose wording that makes each scientific relationship clear while following the selected venue and recipient style choices.

## Source limitations

- These sources support a practical scientific-style reference, not a
  universal writing standard and not a claim about the user's personal voice.
- The writing guides provide expert guidance. They do not experimentally prove
  that every pattern improves comprehension in every scientific genre.
- Reader studies support clarity as an objective but do not justify rigid
  readability scores, sentence-length limits, or paragraph-length quotas.
- Roediger and McDermott is an empirical prose exemplar, not a source of
  universal style rules.
- All task examples and numerical results in this profile are fictional
  teaching illustrations, not the author's studies or observed results,
  unless a source is explicitly attributed.
- Scientific accuracy, source meaning, data, quotations, citations, code,
  structure, and mandated wording remain protected from stylistic alteration.
