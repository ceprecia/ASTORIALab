# Write Introduction

Build the rationale that makes the actual question worth asking.

## Purpose

Draft or revise an introduction for an established research or technical
writing object. Connect the supported problem, relevant knowledge, unresolved
question and present objective. Develop the reader's reasoning path rather
than using citation order as the outline.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md)
completely. Use the [cross-system handoff](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md)
when domain-specialist material or requirements are involved.

Read the relevant MOC by the default in `me.md`, then the sections needed for
this assignment. Read any recipient-supplied writing guidance identified in the brief. If none is supplied, use the document-purpose and evidence requirements and the actual task sources. Writing preferences are not evidence for the project's scientific claims.

Recover the current writing brief: exact document/version, audience, section
purpose, central argument and its provisional or adopted status, any recipient-selected organizational requirements,
source locators, protected terminology, claim ceiling, required structure and
length, and authorized edits. Apply only a writing profile selected for this task or in the recipient's `me.md`. The available references include `writing-style-positive-scientific` and `writing-style-negative-ai`. Apply each independently when selected. Ask only for missing information that changes the
requested wording; continue unaffected work without inventing an answer.

Use the actual research question/objective, current contribution or explicitly
provisional direction, relevant methods/results scope, verified literature
and identified gaps. Obtain any required application or venue headings from
the requirements owner; examples supply style, not current policy.

## Steps

1. Identify the specific question the document addresses and why this audience
   needs its answer. If the central direction is unresolved, ask the smallest
   material question or work on clearly provisional wording as requested.
   Do not select a new scientific gap to make drafting easier.
2. Extract what earlier work establishes, what remains uncertain, and why the
   uncertainty matters for this question. Distinguish an unsupported novelty
   assertion from a documented gap, contradiction, limitation or unresolved test.
3. Follow the recipient- or venue-selected organization. If an outline would help, choose a form that makes the actual rationale and dependencies clear for this audience. Do not assume a topic-sentence chain, funnel or fixed paragraph sequence.
4. Check consequential assertions and inferences against the actual sources and the section's purpose. Explain relationships that the reader needs to evaluate the rationale; a citation alone does not establish support. Select passage structure for the material, without requiring the same rhetorical components in every unit.
5. Supply study details only when they establish support, contrast, scope,
   methodological relevance or a necessary limitation. Compare studies directly
   when their difference matters; otherwise synthesize them by the point they make.
6. Explain the present objective and relevant hypotheses or approach in the
   terminology used by the current work. The proposed test must address the
   stated gap. Distinguish a methodological precedent from evidence that the
   proposed manipulation, model or application will succeed.
7. Revise for audience and length. Use concrete problem, tension or consequence
   supported by the brief to create interest. Do not invent a mechanism,
   construct, reader reaction or generalized significance to strengthen the opening.
8. Reverse-outline the draft and check the evidence-to-question dependencies.
   Check that the final question, Methods/Approach and eventual interpretation
   remain aligned. Report a substantive mismatch instead of smoothing it away.

## Outputs

Return introduction prose and only material unresolved claims/inputs. Include
an outline when requested or useful to resolve the argument before prose.
Identify provisional direction as provisional. Preserve the authorized section
scope; do not rewrite later sections merely to make the introduction consistent.

## Boundaries

- Do not describe a field as having ignored a question without verified support.
- Do not mistake a source collection for an argument or an absence of supplied
  evidence for proof that no evidence exists.
- Do not quietly adopt a scientific interpretation suggested by a reviewer.
- Keep operational description concise here; detailed methods belong to their owner.

## Handoffs

Return to the invoking writing owner. Evidence gaps go to the assigned evidence
or citation owner; consequential framing choices go to the user/domain lead.
Use independent review for consequential reasoning questions as needed, without assuming a named paragraph-method reviewer. The integration editor checks correspondence with the rest of the document. A named method requires recipient selection.

## Checks

- Do the topic sentences reconstruct one coherent rationale?
- Does each evidence cluster support its actual main idea?
- Does the reasoning explain why the cited knowledge makes the question necessary?
- Is the gap evidenced and bounded rather than asserted for novelty?
- Does the closing objective address that gap using current terminology?
