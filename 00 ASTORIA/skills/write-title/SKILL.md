# Write Title

Name the work precisely and make its supported contribution easy to recognize.

## Purpose

Compose or revise a manuscript, proposal, report, or presentation title from
its current subject and intended contribution. Offer different points of entry
when useful, rather than cosmetic synonym swaps. The title compresses the
argument; it does not create a stronger argument than the work supports.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md)
completely. Use the [cross-system handoff](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md)
when domain-specialist material or requirements are involved.

Read the relevant MOC by the default in `me.md`, then the sections needed for
this assignment. Read any recipient-supplied writing guidance identified in the brief. If none is supplied, use the document-purpose and evidence requirements and the actual task sources. Writing preferences are not evidence for the project's scientific claims.

Recover the current writing brief: exact document/version, audience, section
purpose, central argument and its provisional or adopted status, any recipient-selected organizational requirements,
source locators, protected terminology, claim ceiling, required structure and
length, and authorized edits. Apply only a writing profile selected for this task or in the recipient's `me.md`. The available references include `writing-style-positive-scientific` and `writing-style-negative-ai`. Apply each independently when selected. Ask only for missing information that changes the
requested wording; continue unaffected work without inventing an answer.

Also obtain the current abstract or section summary, intended readership,
essential search terms, and any supplied title or character limit. For a
proposal, establish that the title describes intended work rather than a result.

## Steps

1. Extract the subject, studied relationship or problem, population/system,
   and supported contribution from the current document. Separate what is
   established from what is proposed. If the contribution is unresolved,
   use a descriptive title or label contribution-focused candidates provisional.
2. Identify which subject terms are necessary for recognition and search.
   Preserve established construct names; do not replace them with a coined
   umbrella term, fashionable framework, or implied mechanism.
3. Choose a useful title form: descriptive, question, relationship/finding,
   contrast, or method-plus-contribution, according to the brief and evidence.
   A finding title must state an actual supported finding. Avoid a question
   that presupposes an unestablished phenomenon.
4. Draft the requested number of candidates. When no number is given, provide
   only a few meaningfully different candidates. Vary emphasis within the
   same accepted science; explain a consequential tradeoff briefly.
5. Trace every important noun and verb to the document. Test whether causal,
   predictive, neural, mechanistic, clinical, or generalizing language exceeds
   the design or claim ceiling. Repair scope without silently choosing new science.
6. Check length, readability, abbreviation load, venue constraints, and exact
   correspondence with the abstract. Prefer concrete terms over vague novelty
   or significance language. Retain necessary technical specificity.

## Outputs

Return the title or title options first. If useful, distinguish the most
conservative candidate from the candidate that most directly states the
supported contribution. Include only material caveats or unresolved choices.
Do not append an argument dossier to a simple title request. Save only within
the brief's authorized destination and ownership.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent a result, construct, method name, or new comparison to make
  a title sound interesting.
- A catchy phrase cannot carry a claim absent from the manuscript.
- Do not omit a limitation essential to the title's meaning merely to shorten it.
- A title can be descriptive when the scientific contribution is not yet selected.

## Handoffs

Return to the invoking writing owner. Send a missing scientific position to the
user/domain lead; send a needed title-format rule to the requirements or
structure owner. The integration editor checks the final title against the
assembled document; independent content and citation reviewers retain their jobs.

## Checks

- Does the title identify what was actually studied or is actually proposed?
- Does its contribution match the document and central argument?
- Does every implied relationship respect the evidence and inference ceiling?
- Are important construct names and search terms preserved?
- Are alternatives different in emphasis rather than different in science?
