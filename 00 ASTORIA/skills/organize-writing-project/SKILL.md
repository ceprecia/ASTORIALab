# Organize Writing Project

Keep every section attached to its purpose, evidence and next useful action.

## Purpose

Own writing readiness and section coordination for a document or application.
Make gaps, dependencies and owners visible without replacing the existing
project MOC, action tracker or domain planning companion. Organize the full
application when requested, including research, candidate, training, supporting
documents and submission materials.

## Inputs

Read the [writing contract](../writing-coordinator/references/writing_contract.md).
Use the existing MOC, current draft locations, central claim and its status,
writing guidance, selected template, scope, collaborators and known actions.
Get current required sections and constraints from the requirements owner.
Examples establish writing patterns, not current official requirements.

## Steps

1. Identify the canonical document or current draft for each requested
   deliverable. Resolve conflicting versions from current evidence or the user;
   modification time alone does not settle authorship or authority.
2. Inventory required sections from the selected structure or verified
   requirements. Inspect each available section before assigning a state.
   Distinguish absent from not yet inspected.
3. For each section state its reader-facing job and relation to the central
   argument. Candidate, training or administrative sections may serve the
   application's overall case without repeating its scientific hypothesis.
4. Record the minimum useful status: draft location, writing owner, required
   inputs, present material, missing content, next action and blocking dependency.
   Point to existing action records instead of copying their entire task list.
5. Use evidence-based states such as not inspected, missing inputs, outline,
   drafting, review, revision needed or user accepted. A heading, assigned chat
   or assistant statement of completion does not prove readiness.
6. Identify independent work that can proceed while scientific choices remain
   provisional. Do not make completion of a separate project a prerequisite
   unless the specific section depends on an actual missing output.
7. Surface cross-section dependencies and contradictions. Track a changed aim,
   term, method or sample through the sections it affects; leave unaffected
   sections and agreed milestones unchanged.
8. Recommend the next bounded assignments to writing-coordinator, including
   sources, output location, desired completion condition and responsible
   domain owner for missing technical content.
9. If saving is requested, update the existing owning MOC section or existing
   section table. Keep chat IDs and technical tracking in its AI Notes per
   `me.md`. Use capture skills for Research Log entries.

## Outputs

A compact section organizer, scaled to the project:

| Section | Purpose and claim link | Current state and draft | Missing input or decision | Owner and next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include requirement sources, limits and dates only when relevant and verified.
Label planning dates separately from confirmed deadlines. The writing
coordinator uses this organizer; it is not a second independent task system.

## Boundaries

- Do not change aims, hypotheses, technical choices or deadlines by inference.
- Do not shift future milestones when one task is late.
- Do not mark a section accepted without evidence of the user's acceptance.
- Do not create a second MOC, writing dashboard or parallel task list.
- A complete research draft does not establish completion of the full application.

## Handoffs

Return assignments to writing-coordinator and preserve the invoking owner's
project responsibility. keep-writing-structure owns template fidelity.
research-grant-advisor supplies application requirements; other domain owners
use [cross-system handoffs](../writing-coordinator/references/cross_system_handoff.md).
Saving progress routes to capture-progress or quick-capture.

## Checks

- Every required section is represented or explicitly outside scope.
- The current draft and unresolved decisions are identifiable.
- Each next action has a concrete output and its actual dependency.
- The organizer reflects what was inspected, not what was assumed.
